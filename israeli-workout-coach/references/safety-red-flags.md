# Safety Red Flags: Emergency, Urgent, and Route-to-a-Doctor

The Safety section of SKILL.md carries the decision branches. This file carries the
detail behind them. Read it whenever a user reports a symptom, an injury, heat illness,
a pregnancy, diabetes, or distress around food or mood. Nothing here is a diagnosis or a
treatment: every branch ends in "stop training and get the user to the right level of
care", which is the only clinical act this skill performs.

Four tiers, numbered as in SKILL.md. Getting the tier wrong is the expensive error, so when
a symptom fits two tiers, use the more urgent one:

| Tier | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Emergency | Call 101 now | Stop, call מד"א 101, do not keep coaching |
| 2 Urgent | Today, not a routine queue | A doctor today, מוקד, or מיון; not a physiotherapy waiting list |
| 3 Stop and see a doctor | Not 101 if it settles, but no more training until a doctor has looked | End the session; doctor before the next one |
| 4 Routine | Days to weeks is fine | Family or kupah doctor, then physiotherapy per `kupah-physio-routes.md` |

## Tier 1, emergency: call מד"א 101

During or right after training:

- Chest pain or pressure, or pain spreading to the arm or jaw.
- Sudden severe breathlessness.
- Fainting or near-fainting, even with full recovery. NHS guidance lists someone who
  "fainted while exercising" among the cases for an emergency call, because a faint during
  effort can be the first sign of a heart rhythm problem. The gym health declaration also
  asks about loss of consciousness in the past year (question 3), so the gym will want a
  doctor's certificate before they return.
- Sudden one-sided weakness, facial droop, or slurred speech.
- A blow to the head (a dropped bar, a fall from a box) followed by being knocked out, a
  seizure, or being unable to stay awake.
- A racing, pounding, or irregular heartbeat together with chest pain, dizziness,
  fainting, or breathlessness.
- Signs of poor circulation during effort: grey, bluish, or very pale skin, or a cold,
  clammy sweat with dizziness or nausea. Clinical exercise-test stop criteria list "signs of
  poor perfusion: pallor, cyanosis, nausea, or cold and clammy skin" as a reason to stop
  at once.
- A user with diabetes on insulin or a sulfonylurea who becomes confused, cannot swallow,
  or stops responding (severe low blood sugar). Give nothing by mouth; call 101.
- Confusion or collapse in the heat.
- **Sudden, severe, "worst headache of my life" during or right after a maximal or
  heavily-braced effort.** A lifter will rationalise this as a blood-pressure headache
  and rest through it. It is the classic exertional presentation of a subarachnoid
  haemorrhage or an arterial dissection, and it is time-critical. This is why the
  screening rules in SKILL.md cap maximal breath-holding for at-risk users.
- Active suicidal thoughts or intent, disclosed at any point. ERAN 1201 is emotional
  first aid, not an emergency service. If the user says they intend to act, or you
  believe they are in immediate danger, tell them to call 101 or go to the nearest ER,
  and stay with the conversation rather than returning to training talk.

## Tier 2, urgent: today, and not via a physiotherapy referral queue

Most non-soreness pain is a routine doctor-then-physiotherapy matter. These are not, and
sending them into a queue that starts with a GP appointment loses time that matters:

- **Chest pressure, or arm or jaw discomfort, that came on with effort and eased with
  rest**, the first time or in a changed pattern, including when the user mentions it days
  later. That can be angina, which NHS guidance sends for urgent same-day advice when it
  comes and goes, and to an emergency call when it does not stop with rest. So: the same
  day to a מוקד or מיון where an ECG can be done, and no training until seen; if it is
  still present, or returns at rest or at lower effort, it is Tier 1. Chest
  pain during exercise is also question 2 on the Israeli gym health declaration.

- **Suspected tendon or muscle rupture.** An audible pop or snap with immediate loss of
  function, a visible deformity or a lump where the muscle bunched, or inability to bear
  weight. Pec major during bench, distal biceps during a heavy pull or a curl, quadriceps
  or patellar tendon during a squat, and Achilles during jumping or sprinting are the
  classic gym ones, and several are time-sensitive surgical repairs.
- **Neurological signs after a deadlift, squat, or any loaded spinal effort:** numbness
  in the saddle area (inner thighs, buttocks, genitals), a new problem controlling
  bladder or bowel, or weakness in both legs or weakness that is getting worse. This
  picture is treated as a same-day emergency; send them to מיון immediately, do not wait
  for a referral.
- **Suspected fracture:** severe pain with obvious swelling or deformity, or a joint that
  will not take any weight.
- **A hot, red, swollen joint**, especially with a high temperature or feeling shivery.
  NHS guidance treats redness or heat around the knee with a high temperature as a possible
  sign of infection that needs urgent help, not a physiotherapy queue.
- **Pain and swelling in one calf or thigh** (possible deep vein thrombosis): an urgent
  doctor's appointment the same day. With breathlessness or chest pain it is a 101 call.
- **A knee that locks or gives way** after a twist: see a doctor promptly rather than
  training through it.
- **A blow to the head** (a dropped bar, a fall from a box, a contact class) followed by
  vomiting or dizziness, or in someone taking blood thinners: urgent medical advice the
  same day (knockout, seizure, or unable to stay awake is Tier 1). No more training that
  day either way.
- **Possible rhabdomyolysis** (detail below): מיון.

## Tier 3, stop the session and see a doctor before the next one

Not a 101 call when it settles at rest, but the session ends and training does not resume
until a doctor has seen them:

- A racing or irregular heartbeat during exercise that settles with rest (clinical
  exercise-test termination criteria list arrhythmias). With chest pain, dizziness,
  fainting, or breathlessness it is Tier 1.
- Breathlessness or wheezing out of proportion to the effort, or an asthmatic whose
  reliever does not settle it (call 101 if it keeps getting worse).

Say plainly which tier you think this is and why, and that you are not examining them.

## Heat illness: what "cool the person" actually means

Heat exhaustion (heavy sweating, cold clammy skin, dizziness, weakness): stop, rest in a
cool place, cool the body, and give water in small sips if they are fully alert and not
vomiting. The MOH says that if there is no
improvement, or it gets worse with vomiting, reduced alertness, or confusion, get medical
help immediately; NHS guidance expects improvement within about 30 minutes of cooling and
treats someone still unwell after that as heat stroke.

Heat stroke is an emergency (see SKILL.md for the signs). Call 101, then, while waiting:

- Move them to a cool, shaded place and cool the body. The MOH names wet towels; NHS
  guidance adds wrapping them in a cool wet sheet, sponging with cold water, and fanning.
- For exertional heat stroke, the US athletic trainers' position statement (NATA, 2015)
  names cold-water immersion up to the neck as the most effective cooling, in water of
  about 1.7 to 15 degrees. If a tub or container of cold or iced water is right there and
  someone holds the head above water and watches them, immersion while waiting beats
  towels. A summer pool or the sea is usually far warmer than that and adds a drowning
  risk, so do not use them. Otherwise rotate wet ice towels over the body, douse with cold
  water, and fan; NATA calls these less effective than immersion.
- Keep cooling until מד"א arrives.
- **Do not give them anything to drink.** The MOH rule for heat stroke is no drinking at
  all; fluids are given only by the medical team, intravenously.

## Drinking too much is also a risk

The MOH hydration baseline in SKILL.md is a general daily figure, and "drink more in the
heat" needs a ceiling. On long sessions, especially long outdoor runs, drinking well beyond
thirst can dilute blood sodium (exercise-associated hyponatremia), which can look like heat
illness. The 2015 international consensus on it recommends using thirst to guide drinking,
so guide the user to drink to thirst rather than on a schedule. If someone collapses after
drinking heavily through a long session, say that more water is not the answer and that
this needs medical assessment.

## Diabetes

Ask about it in the Step 1 screen and store it in `health_flags`. If the user takes
insulin or a sulfonylurea, exercise can drop blood glucose low, including hours later or
overnight. Stay out of the clinical lane, and give only the standard general advice:
check glucose before and after training, carry fast-acting carbohydrate, avoid training
alone or fasted while doses are being adjusted, and take any dose changes to their doctor
or diabetes nurse.

If symptoms of a low come on mid-session (shaky, sweaty, suddenly weak, confused): stop,
and if they can swallow normally, have them take fast-acting carbohydrate. The American
Diabetes Association's "15/15 rule" is about 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate, then a
recheck after 15 minutes. Training does not resume that session. Confused, unable to
swallow, or unresponsive is Tier 1: nothing by mouth, call 101. Note that at Maccabi an exercise consultation for diabetes is one of
the cases that still requires a doctor's referral.

## Pregnancy and postpartum

Beyond medical clearance and avoiding maximal effort and breath-holding under heavy load,
give the user the standard stop-and-call list. Stop training and contact their doctor or
go to מיון for any of:

- Vaginal bleeding, or fluid leaking.
- Regular painful contractions.
- Calf pain, or swelling in one leg (a clot sign).
- Breathlessness before any exertion, chest pain, or dizziness that does not settle.
- Headache with visual changes.
- Reduced fetal movement.

## Rhabdomyolysis

Severe, unrelenting muscle pain with swelling plus dark brown or cola-coloured urine, or
passing much less urine than usual, after hard or unaccustomed work. Route to מיון, not
to a rest day. Triggers to watch for are not only barbell work: a first high-rep spin or
functional-fitness class, a brutal unaccustomed eccentric session, or a return from a long
break are all typical of the unaccustomed effort behind it.

## Disordered eating and mood

The guardrail and its routing live in SKILL.md. Two additions:

- **Persist the flag.** Once a red flag has appeared, write it into `profile.md`
  `health_flags.eating_disorder_flag` with the date. A guardrail that resets when the
  session ends barely protects anyone, because the pattern it guards against repeats
  across sessions. Only the user clears it, in a later session, by saying so.
- **Escalate on suicidality**, per Tier 1 above.
