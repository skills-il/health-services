# Celiac entitlements in Israel

Sources: Kol-Zchut celiac page https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%A6%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%90%D7%A7
and its sub-pages on education, welfare funding and tax credits; the IDF's own celiac page on
mitgaisim.idf.il (dated 27.11.2022). Kol Zchut blocks plain fetches; it renders in a browser.

## Education

The instrument is the Ministry of Education Director-General standing
instruction **0404 of 01.08.2023, "תלמידים החולים במחלת הצליאק"**. The packaging
rule comes from the nutrition circular of 04.04.2016, section 8.2, and the
parents' health declaration from the circular of 27.08.2013.

Educational institutions are obliged to provide suitable substitutes for
students with celiac in **every educational activity**, inside the institution
**or outside it**, that involves **food or craft materials containing gluten**.
That covers food sales, birthdays, mishloach manot and similar activities.
Institutions where food products are supplied or sold must also ensure an
offering of gluten-free food for students with celiac, and where meals are
served (including a daily meal on a long school day) a suitable portion. The
child takes part in every activity, and the institution must avoid
discrimination or exclusion on medical grounds.

Food portions intended for students with celiac must be:

- packaged in a **different colour**, and
- marked with **special prominent marking**

Parents fill in the **parents' health declaration form** before the start of
the year, stating the child's medical condition; a medical document is
sometimes required.

The Ministry of Education published "שמים לב לצליאק" models in 2026 for gan and
school (for example
https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/briut/2026briut/Celiac/modelceliac-ganim.pdf),
which are worth attaching to a request.

**Practical note.** The colour-and-marking requirement is the anti-mix-up
mechanism and the one most often skipped in practice. Request it explicitly and
in writing. It is also a far more achievable ask than a blanket guarantee of no
cross-contamination, which a school kitchen cannot honestly give.

Note this entitlement is distinct from the Ministry of Education food-allergy
standing instruction, which governs anaphylaxis risk and does not apply here.
Do not cite the allergy circular in a celiac request; it invites the institution
to apply an emergency protocol that does not fit the condition.

## Medication

Two separate points, both frequently missed:

1. Check whether a drug contains **flour or starch originating from wheat**.
   Excipients are where gluten hides in medication.
2. When a celiac patient needs drug treatment but the treatment available in the
   **health basket is not suitable** for celiac patients, there is a **right to
   receive a suitable alternative**.

Raise point 2 with the treating physician and the kupah before paying privately
for an alternative formulation.

## Material assistance (municipal, discretionary)

**Working age.** Celiac patients up to retirement age who are known to and
treated by the **social services department**, and who purchase medicinal or
dedicated food **on a physician's recommendation**, may be eligible for funding
assistance. The assistance is given through the social services department,
subject to budget limits and the priorities set in **each local authority**.

**Retirement age.** Celiac patients who have reached retirement age and need
special nutrition on the basis of a physician's recommendation may likewise be
eligible.

**Published ceiling.** Kol Zchut gives the assistance as up to **1,210 NIS a
month (as of 2026)**, with an income-based co-payment that is never below 10
percent, so the actual aid is between 10 and 90 percent of the ceiling. A family
unhappy with the decision, or without an answer in reasonable time, can appeal
to the Welfare Ministry's district appeals committee.

Both routes are discretionary and budget-bound. Never promise an outcome, and
quote the ceiling only as a maximum before co-payment. The physician's recommendation is the precondition to obtain first, and
being known to the social services department is the second gate.

## Bituach Leumi: set expectations honestly

Celiac does **not** appear as a named qualifying impairment on the Bituach Leumi
disabled-child impairment list, which does name **allergy** as a criterion.

Do not reason by analogy from the food-allergy skill. If a child has an
additional qualifying condition, that is a separate question for the general
Bituach Leumi claim route.

## Income tax

The section 45 credit points for a parent of a child with a disability (2
points) follow, for a child under 18, receipt of or entitlement to the
disabled-child allowance, or one of a few named conditions (for example
blindness, limb paralysis, intellectual disability, special-education
placement). Celiac alone does not open it. This is a negative to state
honestly, not a route to try.

## Subsidised gluten-free flour (national, thin)

The Health Ministry subsidises a gluten-free flour. At the Knesset Health
Committee on 11.05.2026 it was reported that about 1.2 million NIS a year is
allocated and only about 300,000 NIS is used, and the Ministry acknowledged
availability gaps in the retail chains (Maariv, 11.05.2026). Mention it; do not quote a price
or promise availability.

## IDF track

- **Enlistment is compulsory**; celiac recruits used to serve as volunteers.
- Celiac patients receive a **regular enlistment medical profile together with a
  celiac clause**. Per Kol Zchut, absent other medical limitations that means
  **profile 97 with a celiac clause**.
- **Closed, per the IDF page (27.11.2022)**: the submarine flotilla and the fire
  formation (עוצבת האש, the paratroopers brigade and the commando), **except the
  David's Sling formation (עוצבת קלע דוד)**, because of their operational role.
  Kol Zchut (updated July 2025) also lists reconnaissance units as closed, apart
  from pilot, naval officer and naval combat roles.
- **Open**: manoeuvring combat roles were opened to celiacs **on a volunteer
  basis only**, declared in a dedicated preferences questionnaire; nobody is
  assigned there against their will. Kol Zchut names open
  roles including pilot, naval combatant (boys) and recruit-squad commander.
- **Food**: gluten-free meals and bread in the dining halls, gluten-free combat
  rations in the field, gluten-free meals on enlistment day. In reserves an
  "adapted food" indication on the personal record entitles the soldier to
  adapted food and the stipend.
- **Food stipend**: paid to the soldier's bank account, **in addition to** the
  dining-hall provision. The IDF page of 27.11.2022 states **21.9 NIS a day on an
  open base and 34.9 NIS a day on a closed base**. Kol Zchut says the amount may
  change from time to time. No newer published figure was found (search of
  29.10.2026), so always quote it
  with the 27.11.2022 date and tell the soldier to confirm the current rate with
  the unit's welfare NCO (משקית ת"ש).
