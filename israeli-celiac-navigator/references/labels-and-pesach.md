# Labels, cross-contamination and Pesach

## Israeli gluten labelling

On every food product **sold in Israel** that contains gluten, the ingredient
list must show:

- the **name of the plant** that is the source of the gluten, and
- the words **"מכיל גלוטן"**

so that celiac patients know to avoid the product.

Teach users to read for those two elements rather than searching for a
gluten-free logo, which is not what the requirement is built around.

Current labelling requirements sit under the Ministry of Health's guidance on
providing consumers with information about allergens and substances causing
hypersensitivity: https://www.gov.il/he/pages/allergens-guidance

## Practical shopping method

- Read food labels.
- Where a product is unclear, clarify with the **manufacturer** that it does not
  contain gluten, and obtain the confirmation **in writing**.

## The grains

Gluten-containing: **wheat, barley, rye**, and **oats**, the last being the
contested one. Leumit lists oat products (quaker, granola, oat matza, oat bran)
among those to avoid.

**Disputed by contamination, not composition**: quinoa, teff, amaranth and
sorghum. They are probably safe in themselves, but there is a real concern about
contamination with gluten-containing grains **during harvest or milling**. This
is a supply-chain risk, not a property of the grain, which is why manufacturer
confirmation matters more than the botanical name.

## Pesach: two errors in opposite directions

**Error 1, the dangerous one: treating "kosher for Passover" as "gluten free".**
Not everything kosher for Passover is suitable for people with celiac. **Matza
flour** is the explicit counterexample.

**Error 2, the wasteful one: avoiding Passover products entirely.** Products
that are kosher for Passover and do **not** contain matza flour or Passover
flour **are** suitable for celiac. This is why the shelves are unusually
friendly during the season, and why Pesach is genuinely a good stocking-up
period for a celiac household.

**The vocabulary trap.** Kitniyot is a **kashrut** category, not a gluten
category:

| | Kashrut concept | Gluten status |
|---|---|---|
| Chametz | Leavened grain, a kashrut category | Overlaps with gluten grains but is not the same test |
| Kitniyot | Legumes and rice, permitted or not by custom | Says nothing at all about gluten |
| Matza flour | Kosher for Passover | **Contains gluten** |

A kitniyot-free product may still contain gluten. A kitniyot product may be
perfectly gluten free. Never let an agent merge the two vocabularies.

**The rule to give a family:** at Pesach, read specifically for matza flour and
Passover flour, and do not treat the holiday label as a safety certificate.
