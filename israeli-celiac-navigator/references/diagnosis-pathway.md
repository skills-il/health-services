# Celiac diagnosis pathway in Israel

Primary source: Schneider Children's Medical Center,
https://www.schneider.org.il/?CategoryID=832&ArticleID=2439

## The rule that has to come first

A gluten-free diet begun **before** the disease is diagnosed by biopsy of the
duodenal lining may mislead and make diagnosis more difficult. In the standard
pathway the child eats a menu containing gluten for several months before the
test, and **gluten must not be avoided before the biopsy**.

Everything else in this file is downstream of that sentence. If a family is
still at the suspicion stage, the order is: test first, change diet after.

## When the family has already gone gluten-free

This is common and it is not a catastrophe, but it must be disclosed to the
physician at the referral, because it changes how results are interpreted.

**Do not design the reintroduction.** Whether a gluten challenge is needed, for
how long, and at what quantity, is a medical decision belonging to the treating
physician, and for a child to a paediatric gastroenterologist. The skill's job
is to make sure the family raises it, not to prescribe it.

## Serology

The most sensitive antibodies for celiac are of the **IgA** group:

- antibodies against **TTG**
- antibodies against **endomysium**

Because these antibodies are IgA, a state of **IgA deficiency**, which is more
common among celiac patients, affects the serology. Total IgA is therefore
checked alongside. A single negative antibody result should not be read as a
definitive all-clear without knowing the IgA status.

## Biopsy

Diagnostic criteria rest on a biopsy showing appropriate findings.

A **further** biopsy is required when:

- the first diagnosis was not certain, or
- the first diagnosis was made **before age two**

Parents of toddlers diagnosed very early frequently assume the matter is
permanently settled. It may not be.

## Prevalence

Celiac reaches about **one percent** of the population in adults and children.
An Israeli study by Prof. Raanan Shamir, Prof. Avraham Elikim, Prof. Aharon
Lerner, Prof. Shenar and colleagues found a prevalence of at least **1:157**,
and apparently that is an underestimate of the true prevalence.

Useful for reassuring a family who feels they are dealing with something
vanishingly rare. They are not.

## What this pathway is NOT

Celiac is autoimmune. There is no EpiPen, no anaphylaxis pathway, no adrenaline
and no emergency protocol. A user describing airway symptoms after eating is
describing IgE food allergy, which is a different condition with a different
skill (`israeli-food-allergy-navigator`) and, if it is happening in the moment,
a different response entirely: adrenaline and 101.
