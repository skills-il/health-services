# Patient Rights: Emergency Routes and Involuntary Admission

This file carries the two areas where an agent most often gives a confidently wrong answer: which
emergency door to send someone to, and what happens once an involuntary admission is on the table.

## Emergency routes, and which rules apply

| Route | Referral needed? | Cost | Hours |
|-------|------------------|------|-------|
| Psychiatric hospital emergency room | No | Free | 24 hours a day, all year |
| General hospital emergency room | No | An ER fee applies. A Form 17 commitment (hitchayvut) from your kupah reduces it; a specific referral to that medical centre from an authorized party TOGETHER WITH a Form 17 commitment avoids it; admission waives it entirely. The Ministry of Health page states all three, so give all three | 24/7 |
| Community mental health clinic | Yes, from a family doctor or a mental health clinician. Some kupot also require a pre-authorization request | Depends on the setting, see SKILL.md Step 2 | Clinic hours |

The referral and pre-authorization rules that govern the community route do NOT govern the
emergency route. A person in acute distress should not be told to obtain a referral first.

## Getting a refusing person examined: the way IN

The tables below start at an order that already exists. This section is how one comes to exist,
which is what a family whose relative refuses to be seen actually needs. The addressee is the
DISTRICT PSYCHIATRIST (psikh'iater mechozi), not the police and not the emergency room, and any
person may bring the case to them.

| Order | Conditions, all of which must be met | Section |
|-------|--------------------------------------|---------|
| Urgent compulsory examination | The person is ill and as a result their judgment or their capacity to test reality is substantially impaired; AND they are liable to place themselves or another in IMMEDIATE physical danger; AND they have refused to be examined by a psychiatrist | s.6(a) |
| Urgent compulsory examination of a minor | On the application of a social worker under the Youth (Care and Supervision) Law, where there is prima facie evidence the minor has a mental illness or disturbance liable to endanger themselves or another physically. The examination must be carried out by a child and adolescent psychiatrist | s.6(b), s.6(c) |
| Non-urgent compulsory examination | Illness substantially impairing judgment or reality-testing; AND one of: non-immediate physical danger to self or others, severely impaired ability to meet basic needs, causing severe mental suffering to another in a way that disrupts their normal way of life, or serious damage to property; AND refusal to be examined | s.7 |
| Validity of an examination order | 10 days from the day it is given | s.8 |

A hospitalization order can only follow the examination, not precede it.

## Involuntary admission, Treatment of Mental Patients Law, 1991

Compulsory care runs on two clocks that secondary summaries routinely merge: how long the ORDER is
valid, and how long the HOSPITALIZATION it authorizes may last.

| Stage | Rule | Section |
|-------|------|---------|
| District psychiatrist's hospitalization order | The order is in force for 10 days from issue | s.9(c) |
| Initial hospitalization under that order | Up to 7 days | s.10(a) |
| First extension | The district psychiatrist may extend by a further 7 days | s.10 |
| Further extensions | Only by a psychiatric committee: first for a period of up to 3 months, then for further periods of up to 6 months each | s.10 |

Safeguards, which are the operative part for a user asking what they can do:

| Right | Rule | Section |
|-------|------|---------|
| Appeal to the psychiatric committee | Any person may appeal. An appeal against a hospitalization order is heard within 5 days; against a compulsory outpatient-treatment order, within 10 days | s.12 |
| Composition of the committee | A panel of three: a member of the jurists' list as chair, plus two psychiatrists | s.24 |
| Appeal to the District Court | Within 45 days of the decision being delivered | s.29 |
| Right to counsel | A patient hospitalized under a hospitalization order is entitled to be represented by a lawyer appointed under the Legal Aid Law, 1972 | s.29A |

Source for every row above: the full text of the law (use the action=raw view for exact wording) at
https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7_%D7%98%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%95%D7%9C_%D7%91%D7%97%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%99_%D7%A0%D7%A4%D7%A9

Do not quote a number of days from memory or from a secondary summary. The two clocks in the table
above are the ones that get swapped.

## Sal shikum: the mental health rehabilitation basket

A separate Ministry of Health entitlement, at a lower bar than the disability pension. Israeli
residents aged 18 and over with at least 40% psychiatric disability qualify, determined either by
Bituach Leumi or by a psychiatrist authorized by the Ministry of Health. There is no pension test
and no earning-capacity test, so a person refused the general disability pension entirely can still
be eligible. Application is to a regional rehabilitation committee.

Covered services include housing (hostels, supported and assisted living), employment, completion
of education, dental treatment, leisure, treatment coordination, initial-equipment funding, social
mentorship, and family counselling centres. Your outpatient clinic is obliged to help you prepare
the committee application, and that assistance is itself part of the health basket.

Do not merge this 40% threshold with the general disability pension's thresholds (at least 60%
medical disability, or at least 40% where one impairment reaches 25%). They are different bars set
by different bodies for different entitlements, and merging them denies a real entitlement to
someone who qualifies for one and not the other.
