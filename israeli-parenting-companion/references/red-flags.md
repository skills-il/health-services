# Red flags: when not to wait for the next visit

Every line below is quoted from a Ministry of Health (MoH) page, or from Maccabi where marked, checked on 2026-11-17. Quote the rule and the route; never judge whether a child's symptom "counts". When in doubt, the parent calls the kupah nurse line, goes to the ER, or calls MDA 101.

## ER now, or MDA 101

Source: https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/common-childhood-diseases-and-symptoms/viral-diseases/meningitis/

| Hebrew (MoH) | English |
|---|---|
| קושי להעיר את הילד או הילדה, חוסר תגובה או אובדן הכרה. | Hard to wake, not responding, or loss of consciousness. |
| בלבול משמעותי או שינוי חריג במצב ההכרה. | Marked confusion or an unusual change in consciousness. |
| פרכוסים. | Seizures. |
| פריחה אדומה או סגולה שלא נעלמת בלחיצה, במיוחד לצד חום או הרגשה כללית רעה. | A red or purple rash that does not fade when pressed, especially with fever or feeling very unwell. |
| קשיי נשימה או עור חיוור ומנומר, לצד סימנים למחלה קשה. | Breathing difficulty or pale, mottled skin with signs of severe illness. |

In babies the clearest sign may be a significant change in behaviour, eating or alertness (same page).

## Fever by age

Sources: the meningitis page above and https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/common-childhood-diseases-and-symptoms/fever/high-temperature-and-fever/

| Age | MoH rule |
|---|---|
| Up to 2 months | 38 or higher: ER immediately, even with no other symptoms. Above 38.5 no referral is needed and the ER visit is exempt from payment. A referral at any hour comes from the kupah's phone line. |
| 2 to 3 months | 38 or higher: urgent medical check. With floppiness, unusual sleepiness or reduced alertness: ER now. |
| 2 months to 1 year | MoH general guidance: look at drinking and behaviour, and if the fever does not pass, see a doctor. This is the Ministry's general rule, not a judgement that a particular baby can wait; when unsure, call the kupah nurse line. |
| Any age | Breathing difficulty or apathy: medical check. No improvement after 48 hours: medical check. |

High fever is defined by the Ministry as above 38. For 2 months to 1 year, quote the rule as the Ministry's general guidance; it never clears a specific child to wait.

## Low temperature and jaundice

- Below 36 in a baby can be a sign of illness: warm them (an extra layer, a blanket, held close). If warming does not help and the child looks ill, get a medical check (https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/common-childhood-diseases-and-symptoms/fever/high-temperature-and-fever/).
- Marked jaundice, for example in the whites of the eyes: do not wait for the follow-up date, go for a check now. Bilirubin usually peaks on day 5, after most babies are discharged, so follow the discharge letter's follow-up instructions (https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/family-planning/labor/neonatal-jaundice/).

## Seizure

Source: https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/common-childhood-diseases-and-symptoms/medical-conditions/convulsions/

- Lay the child on the side with the head to the side, move dangerous objects away. Do not open the mouth, pull the tongue, or try to stop the seizure.
- Call MDA if it does not stop within 5 minutes, or if it includes breathing difficulty, choking or blue skin.
- Maccabi adds: a seizure, with or without fever, needs urgent medical help (https://www.maccabi4u.co.il/healthguide/emergencies/emergency_situations/kids_emergencies/kids_convulsion/).

## Breathing (babies and toddlers)

Source: https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/common-childhood-diseases-and-symptoms/respiratory-system/bronchiolitis/

- Fast breathing: more than 60 breaths a minute under age 1.
- Respiratory distress: the skin between the ribs sinks in when breathing, nasal flaring.
- Either one: immediate medical check or the ER.

## Vomiting, diarrhea, dehydration

Sources: https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/common-childhood-diseases-and-symptoms/digestive-system/vomiting/ and https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/common-childhood-diseases-and-symptoms/digestive-system/diarrhea/

- Dehydration signs: little urine, dark urine, dry mouth, no response, sunken fontanelle.
- Bloody or green vomit: ER immediately.
- See a doctor: vomiting over 24 hours, dehydration, strong abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea, yellowish skin or eyes.
- Diarrhea: infants and toddlers over 48 hours, children over 72 hours, blood or mucus in the stool, fever above 39, many vomits, or dehydration signs. Keep breastfeeding or formula.

## Medicine, allergy, shaking

- Too much of a medicine: National Poison Information Center 04-7771900, any hour (https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/common-childhood-diseases-and-symptoms/fever/antipyretic-therapy-in-children/).
- Anaphylaxis: adrenaline injection, then the ER within a quarter of an hour (https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/common-childhood-diseases-and-symptoms/medical-conditions/anaphylactic-shock/).
- "Crying does not kill, shaking does." Put the baby in the crib, go to another room, come back every few minutes, and ask someone trusted to take over for a short break (https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/early-childhood-care/different-types-of-crying/).

## Measles contact

If the child (or anyone in the family) was in contact with someone who has measles, call Kol HaBriut *5400 or the district health bureau as soon as possible. By age and vaccination status they decide whether a vaccine is needed (https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/common-childhood-diseases-and-symptoms/viral-diseases/measles/).

## After a vaccine

Source: https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/immunization-schedule/children-vaccines/rsv-vaccine-beyfortus/

- Contact the kupah if the baby seems apathetic, a fever does not pass after 24 to 48 hours, or appetite changes do not pass after 24 to 48 hours.
- Up to 2 months, any fever over 38, with or without a vaccine, means contacting the doctor or going to the ER.
- In the week after rotavirus dose 1 or 2: abdominal pain with extreme crying, vomiting, blood in the stool, legs drawn to the chest, or weakness means the ER now (https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/immunization-schedule/children-vaccines/rotavirus-vaccine/).

## Non-urgent questions

Tipat Halav nurse line at Kol HaBriut *5400, extension 1 then 9. Sunday to Thursday 16:00 to 21:00, Friday 08:00 to 13:00. From abroad 972-8-6241010. Free for parents of children from birth to age 6 (https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/infant-and-child-development/tipat-halav/tipat-halav/).
