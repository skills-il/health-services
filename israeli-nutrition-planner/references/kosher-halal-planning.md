# Kosher and Halal Meal Planning

Companion reference for `israeli-nutrition-planner`. Load when the household keeps
either. Always ask which practice and which custom, never infer it from a name or a city.

## Kosher: meat and dairy

| Rule | Practical impact on meal planning |
|------|----------------------------------|
| **Meat and dairy cannot be eaten together** | Plan each meal as meat (basari), dairy (halavi), or neutral (pareve) |
| **Waiting after meat before dairy** | 3 to 6 hours depending on community custom, with 6 hours the most common practice in Israel and 3 hours the Yemenite custom. Space dairy meals accordingly |
| **Waiting after dairy before meat** | Typically 30 minutes to 1 hour; after hard cheese, 6 hours |
| **Separate utensils** | Recipes should state which kitchen set to use |
| **Pareve foods** | Eggs, fish, fruits, vegetables, grains and legumes go with either meat or dairy |

Practical consequences: breakfast is naturally dairy-friendly; a meat lunch pushes a dairy
dinner at least 6 hours later; Friday night is usually meat and Saturday lunch is family
dependent; and a pareve menu removes the timing problem entirely.

## Pesach

Chametz removal takes whole grains out of the green band for eight days. Kitniyot removal,
which applies in Ashkenazi practice but generally not in Sephardi and Mizrahi practice,
also takes legumes out of the yellow band. Ask which custom applies before planning, and
see SKILL.md Step 7 for the Ashkenazi eight-day protein and fibre plan. Quinoa is
permitted by many but not all rabbinic authorities, so ask rather than assume.

## Halal

| Constraint | Practical impact |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Halal meat and poultry** | Ask which certification the household accepts. Many kosher-certified products are acceptable to halal-observant families, but never assume it |
| **No pork** | Exclude pork in every form, including gelatin and fats derived from it |
| **No alcohol** | Exclude cooking wine, alcohol-containing sauces, vanilla extract, and any vinegar the household treats as alcoholic |
| **Gelatin and enzymes** | Check gelatin in yogurts and desserts, and rennet in cheese, for animal source |
| **No meat-dairy rule** | There is no meat-dairy separation, so combined dishes are open and the menu is wider than a kosher one |

### Building on the existing kitchen

Arab-Israeli home cooking already sits close to the Nutritional Rainbow: mujaddara,
freekeh and burghul, molokhia and other cooked greens, ful and hummus, labneh, olive oil,
zaatar, fresh salads, and baked or grilled fish and poultry. Anchor the plan in those
dishes. The realistic levers are sweetened drinks, portions of white rice and refined
bread, frying, and the sodium load of commercial spice mixes and pickles. Offer the plan
in Arabic where the user prefers it.
