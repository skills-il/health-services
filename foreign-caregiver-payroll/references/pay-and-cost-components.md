# Pay and Cost Components

Every rate and table used to compute the monthly cost of a foreign caregiver employed in a
private home, with sources. Figures are current as of mid-2026; verify against the linked
pages before quoting for back-pay or a dispute.

## Wage floor

| Item | Value | Effective |
|------|-------|-----------|
| Monthly minimum wage (full-time) | 6,443.85 NIS | 01.04.2026 |
| Hourly minimum wage | 35.40 NIS | 01.04.2026 |

A live-in caregiver is paid a monthly global wage. The Hours of Work and Rest Law does not
apply (Yolanda Gluten ruling), so there is no hourly overtime. The often-cited "minimum wage
plus 20%" is a non-binding dissent, not a statutory entitlement.

Weekly rest is 25 continuous hours; work during the weekly rest is paid at 150%.

Source: https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/שכר_מינימום and
https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/תנאי_העסקה_של_עובד_זר_בסיעוד_המועסק_בבית_המטופל

| Component | Rate / cap | Notes |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| National Insurance | 3.6% of wage | From January 2025 (was 2%). The 5,500 NIS reduced ceiling ended 03.08.2022; 3.6% now applies to the full wage |
| In lieu of pension | 6.5% of wage | Owed to the worker and accruing only FROM THE SEVENTH MONTH. ## Housing deduction (changed 1.9.2026)

From 1.9.2026 (Foreign Workers (Rate of Wage Deductions for Suitable Accommodation) (Amendment)
Regulations 5786-2026, published 16.7.2026) the employer may deduct, as a reimbursement of housing
expenses it actually incurred or committed to, at most this regional ceiling for a permanent
building: Jerusalem 1,095, Tel Aviv 1,257, Haifa 999, Centre 1,030, South 999, North 999 NIS/month.
The ceiling includes arnona, water, electricity, furniture and electrical appliances. The separate
utilities line (94.34 for a nursing caregiver in the employer's home) and the half rate for
employer-owned housing (Jerusalem 254.31, Tel Aviv 289.18, Haifa/centre 192.81, south 171.40,
north 157.71) applied only until 31.8.2026, so use them for back-pay on January to August 2026. The
amounts are re-indexed every 1 January (80% rent index, 20% CPI). How the "actually incurred"
expense is measured when the worker lives in the family's own flat is not settled by the text.

Source: https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/ניכוי_הוצאות_מגורים_לעובד_זר and
https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_עובדים_זרים_(שיעור_ניכויים_מהשכר_בעד_מגורים_הולמים)

## Deposit fund: the home-employer exemption

The standard foreign-worker deposit is 12.5% of wages (6% in lieu of severance + 6.5% in lieu of
contributions) into a central fund. The exemption is the INTERSECTION of two conditions: the
nursing sector AND employment by an individual otherwise than in the course of that individual's
business or occupation. A care company employing the same worker still deposits, and where the
patient and a company employ the worker in parallel each side carries its pro-rata share.

What the exempt home employer owes instead is a sum paid to the worker at the END of employment
(or monthly into a separate account in the worker's name). A foreign worker cannot be insured in
an Israeli pension fund, so nothing is remitted anywhere and nothing is withheld from the worker.
The amount depends on how the employment ended:

| How employment ends | Amount owed |
|---------------------|-------------|
| Resigns, or dismissed before completing one year | 12.5% of monthly wage (6% severance component + 6.5% in lieu of contributions), for months **from the seventh month onward** |
| Severance is triggered: dismissed after a year, the employer dies, or a resignation carrying severance | **8.33%** of monthly wage x every month worked **from month 1**, PLUS **6.5%** for months **from the seventh month onward** |

Three practical points. The 6.5% leg starts in month 7, so reserving it from month 1 over-reserves
the first half-year. 8.33% is one twelfth, so a severance reserve of wage / 12 is the right
magnitude, and it should be topped up after any raise because severance is computed on the last
salary. And the whole thing is conditional on the exit route, so a worker who resigns inside the
first year receives the 12.5% figure rather than full severance.

## Sick days (dmei machala)

The worker accrues 1.5 sick days per month (18 a year), up to a maximum of 90. The first day of
illness is unpaid, the second and third days are paid at 50% of wage, and from the fourth day
onward the full wage is paid.

Source: https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/דמי_מחלה

Source: https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/הפקדת_פיקדון_חודשי_עבור_עובדים_זרים and

## Recuperation pay (havraa)

Tariff: **451.50 NIS per day** (private sector) for havraa-year 2026, i.e. 1.7.2025 to 30.6.2026. Updated 18.08.2026 RETROACTIVELY: an employer who already paid this havraa year at the previous 418 NIS owes the differential. Public sector is higher. Payment becomes due only after 12 full months, but it is then paid RETROACTIVELY from the worker's first day, so the liability ACCRUES from month one and must be reserved monthly (kolzchut/דמי_הבראה: "זכאי לתשלום דמי הבראה רטרואקטיבית, החל מיום עבודתו הראשון"). Re-read the source every cycle; this figure has moved mid-year.

| Year of employment | Days |
|--------------------|------|
| 1st | 5 |
| 2nd-3rd | 6 |
| 4th-10th | 7 |
| 11th-15th | 8 |
| 16th-19th | 9 |
| 20th onward | 10 |

The row is the year being worked, not years completed: after one completed year the worker is in
the 2nd year and accrues 6 days (Kol Zchut's worked example: "6 ימי הבראה עבור השנה השנייה").
As of 19.10.2026 no tariff has been published for the havraa-year that began 1.7.2026; Kol Zchut
still gives 451.50.

Source: https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/דמי_הבראה

## Annual leave (6-day work week)

Leave seniority counts CALENDAR years (the Annual Leave Law's work year starts on 1 January), not
anniversaries: the row is the number of calendar years in which the employment existed, including
the current one, and the first year is prorated. Kol Zchut publishes TWO columns. Gross includes the weekly rest day; NET is the number of days
the worker is actually absent, and leave redemption (pidyon chufsha) is computed on the NET
figure. Quoting the gross column alone over-states the liability.

| Calendar year of employment | Net days absent | Gross days |
|-----------|-----------------|------------|
| Years 1-5 | 14 | 16 |
| Year 6 | 16 | 18 |
| Year 7 | 18 | 21 |
| Year 8 | 19 | 22 |
| Year 9 | 20 | 23 |
| Year 10 | 21 | 24 |
| Year 11 | 22 | 25 |
| Year 12 | 23 | 26 |
| Year 13 | 24 | 27 |
| Year 14+ | 24 | 28 |

Source: https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/חישוב_מספר_ימי_החופשה_השנתית_לעובדים_במקומות_עבודה_שבהם_מונהג_שבוע_עבודה_בן_6_ימים

## Holidays by country of origin

The caregiver is entitled to pay for 9 holidays a year and may choose their own religion's
holidays instead of the Israeli holidays. The choice is made once and is fixed for the whole
employment.

Source: https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/תנאי_העסקה_של_עובד_זר_בסיעוד_המועסק_בבית_המטופל

## Long-term-care (nursing) benefit offset

Paid in 6 levels, and Bituach Leumi publishes TWO columns. Employing a FOREIGN caregiver: L1 1,708 / L2 2,484 / L3 3,478 / L4 4,471 / L5 5,465 / L6 6,458. Employing an ISRAELI caregiver: L1 1,708 / L2 2,484 / L3 4,223 / L4 5,217 / L5 6,458 / L6 7,452. Use the foreign column for this skill, and note that 6,458 sits in both at different levels
(as of 01.08.2026; the foreign column was 1,705 / 2,480 / 3,472 / 4,464 / 5,456 / 6,448 from 01.04.2026). The recipient may employ a foreign caregiver directly (cash) or through a
care company. Subtract the relevant level from the gross monthly cost to estimate net cost.

Source: https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/Long_Term_Care/Pages/ovedZar.aspx and
https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/Long_Term_Care/Pages/money.aspx
