# Employment Process: Foreign Caregiver in a Private Home

This reference details the legal steps to employ a foreign nursing caregiver directly in a
private home. It does NOT cover caregivers employed by a care company (the company carries the
obligations in that case).

## 1. Eligibility (who may employ)

A permit is granted only for a relative who is not in an institution and needs essentially
full-time care. Eligible groups include:

- Seniors aged 85 and over.
- Recipients of the special-services benefit (sherutim meyuchadim, "שר\"מ") at a rate below 112%.
- Work-injury victims and cancer patients.
- A child with a disability.

The list above is not exhaustive; confirm the recipient's specific condition against the
Population Authority service page and the Kol Zchut permit page.

## 2. The employment permit (heter haasaka)

- Apply to the Population and Immigration Authority (Rashut HaUchlusin VeHaHagira).
- Employing a foreign worker without a valid permit is illegal.
- Some applications are referred to a professional advisory committee, and the decision is sent
  by registered mail.

## 3. Registering with a licensed private bureau (lishka pratit)

- After the permit, both the employer and the worker must be registered with one of the
  licensed private placement bureaus for the nursing sector.
- The bureau assists with the permit process, work visas, and handles cases of worker illness
  or death.
- This channel is mandatory for the nursing sector.

## 4. Mandatory medical insurance

- The employer must provide private medical insurance from a licensed insurer for the whole
  employment period (foreign workers are outside the National Health Insurance Law).
- The employer may deduct from wages at most half of the insurance cost, capped at 154.29 NIS
  per month.
