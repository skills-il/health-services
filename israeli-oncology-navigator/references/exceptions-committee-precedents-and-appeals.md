# Exceptions Committee Refusals -- Internal Appeals and Labor Court Precedents

> Disclaimer: bureaucracy / legal-overview reference, not legal advice. All case names and rulings are summaries from public Israeli sources. Always consult an attorney before filing court action.

## The escalation ladder when an HMO refuses funding

When an HMO (Clalit, Maccabi, Meuhedet, Leumit) refuses to fund an out-of-basket or off-label cancer drug, the patient has a structured set of escalation steps. Most patients **skip the cheap and fast steps** and go straight to court. Don't.

### Step 1: Internal HMO appeals committee (ועדת ערר / בירורים)

Every HMO has an internal appeals / inquiries body for member disputes. Clalit explicitly markets theirs as **fast, neutral, free of charge** -- no court fees, no lawyer required.

- **What it is**: an internal review of the original refusal, before any external escalation
- **Who can file**: the member (or family member with authorization)
- **Cost**: free
- **Use when**: you have a written refusal in hand and want to challenge it without going to court yet

Source: Clalit appeals page (ועדת בירורים) -- https://www.clalit.co.il/he/info/Pages/berurim.aspx

### Step 2: MOH Public Complaints Commissioner for the National Health Insurance Law

If the HMO's internal committee rejects the appeal (or doesn't respond in a reasonable time), the next escalation is the **Ministry of Health Public Complaints Commissioner (נציב קבילות הציבור לחוק ביטוח בריאות ממלכתי)**.

- **2024 volume**: 12,809 inquiries received; 4,399 classified as substantive complaints
- **Per-10,000-members complaint rates (2024)**: Meuhedet 4.47, Maccabi 4.22, Leumit lowest, Clalit highest absolute volume but improving

Source (data): https://www.ynet.co.il/health/article/skfpiq005kl

### Step 3: Regional Labor Court (בית הדין האזורי לעבודה)

If steps 1 and 2 fail, the next venue is the Regional Labor Court -- the correct court under the National Health Insurance Law for HMO-funding disputes.

- Patients can file a regular claim, OR an **interim order (צו ביניים / סעד זמני)** when life is at stake
- Interim orders can issue within days when the medical evidence is urgent
- A further appeal lies on legal grounds only to the National Labor Court
- HMO bears the burden to justify a refusal of medically-recommended treatment

## The governing doctrine

Israeli Labour Court case law establishes the procedural standard applied in HMO drug-funding disputes: **a drug's exclusion from the basket is not by itself grounds for refusal**. The exceptions committee must (a) evaluate clinical efficacy for the individual patient, (b) consider whether basket alternatives exist, and (c) support any budget argument with calculations rather than assertions.

### Other patient-funding rulings

Israeli labour courts continue to rule on HMO drug-funding disputes. Case names must be pulled from Nevo or Takdin by an attorney. The workhorse doctrine cited in current cases is the one above: basket-exclusion alone is not grounds for refusal, and the HMO bears the burden of justifying one.

## Building the appeals package

Whether for the internal HMO committee or for court, the package the patient (or attorney) builds is roughly the same:

1. **The original written refusal letter** from the HMO
2. **Treating physician's detailed recommendation** with:
   - Clinical justification specific to the patient
   - Medical literature supporting the treatment
   - Documentation that basket alternatives have been tried OR are contraindicated
3. **Full medical records** -- diagnosis, staging, treatment history, imaging, pathology
4. **Patient's own statement** of how the treatment matters (psychosocial, prognosis)
5. **For court only**: legal argument citing the precedents above

## Common mistakes to avoid

1. **Skipping the internal HMO committee** and going straight to court -- costs time and money you don't need to spend
2. **Filing the court case without first getting a written refusal** from the HMO -- the court will dismiss for lack of justiciable dispute
3. **Letting the 30-day Bituach Leumi appeal window pass** while focusing only on the HMO drug fight -- these are separate clocks
4. **Citing the wrong court** -- the Regional Labor Court is correct under National Health Insurance Law, not Magistrates' Court
5. **Not asking for an interim order (צו ביניים)** when life is at stake -- a regular claim can take months
