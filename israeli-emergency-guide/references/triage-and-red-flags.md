**Red flags that override the table and send the user to 101 or an ER, not to Terem or a clinic:**
- **Any fever in an infant under 3 months.** This needs a hospital sepsis workup, which an urgent-care clinic cannot do. The Ministry of Health's own ER exemption list singles out this group.
- A non-blanching rash (press a glass against it: if it does not fade, suspect meningococcal sepsis), neck stiffness, or a bulging fontanelle in a baby.
- Fever in a child with a known immune deficiency, or in an oncology patient who may be neutropenic.
- Altered consciousness after a head injury, or any head injury in a person taking anticoagulants.
- Anaphylaxis. See `references/first-aid-basics.md`.

**A first-ever "panic attack" is a medical rule-out before it is a mental-health call.** Chest pain, palpitations, breathlessness and a sense of impending doom are also how a heart attack, a pulmonary embolism, an arrhythmia, hypoglycaemia and thyrotoxicosis present. Call 101 first, and route to a crisis line second, when it is a first-ever episode, when there is true chest pain, in anyone over 40 or with cardiac risk factors, or after recent surgery, immobility or pregnancy. See Step 7.

**Shabbat and chagim.** Hospital ERs and MDA run normally. Pikuach nefesh (פיקוח נפש) overrides Shabbat prohibitions, so calling 101 for a suspected emergency is not merely permitted but required, and hesitation over Shabbat is a real and documented source of delay: say so plainly to an observant user. Kupat cholim clinics are closed from Friday afternoon to Saturday night, which is the largest routine after-hours gap in the week; prescriptions go through the on-duty pharmacy roster (תורנות בתי מרקחת). Yom Kippur is the outlier: roads are closed to traffic so ambulances take longer to reach you, and the 118 welfare line is the one emergency number that shuts that day.

**What the ambulance costs.** MDA transport is billed, and the bill arrives weeks later. Refunds and waivers exist: transport that ends in admission for a qualifying condition, cardiac events, road-accident casualties billed through the insurer, and casualties of hostile action through the National Insurance Institute. Keep the transport documentation and claim through your kupah. Never let cost deter a 101 call.

## Mass-casualty events (aran, אר"ן)

In a bombing, a ramming, a rocket impact with casualties, or a multi-vehicle crash, the rules a
civilian most often gets wrong are these:

- **Do not drive casualties to hospital yourself.** Private cars converging on the scene block the
  access route MDA and the fire service need, and they arrive at an ER that has not been told what
  is coming.
- **Do not congregate at the scene.** Secondary devices and secondary attackers target the crowd
  that gathers. Move away and stay away.
- The scene is a crime scene. Do not move anything you do not have to move to save a life.
- **Do not call 101 for information.** The dispatch line must stay clear for the incident.
  Casualty tracing and hospital status run through the Ministry of Health information line
  (`*5400`) and the hospitals' own information centres.
- If you are uninjured and trained, offer yourself to the incident commander on scene rather than
  starting your own treatment.

## Uninsured, unregistered and newly arrived people

The Patient Rights Law obliges emergency treatment in a medical emergency regardless of insurance
status. Read the scope of that duty carefully before repeating it to a user:

- It covers **emergency** treatment. It does not oblige non-urgent care, it does not prevent
  billing afterwards, and it does not stop a hospital asking a non-resident for a deposit before a
  non-emergency admission.
- Never phrase it as "you will be treated free" or "they cannot bill you". Phrase it as: emergency
  treatment must be given, and you will still receive a bill.

Populations that hit this and are not tourists: an oleh still inside the waiting period before
kupah cover begins, an asylum seeker or foreign worker with no kupah, and an Israeli whose kupah
membership has lapsed. Practical routes for them are Terem's self-pay tariff, the clinics that
serve migrants and asylum seekers, and Ministry of Health humanitarian arrangements. A tourist
should be routed through travel insurance.

## Mental-health escalation ladder

**Psychiatric ER (מלר"ד פסיכיאטרי): walk-in, 24/7, no referral needed, and free of charge.** This is the missing rung between a chat line and the police, and most users do not know it exists. Anyone attending a mental-health medical centre's ER pays nothing (חוזר מנכ"ל 06/2024)
- Each of the four kupot cholim runs its own 24/7 mental-health line; the Ministry of Health's crisis-support page carries the current numbers. Check there rather than quoting a shortcode from memory
- Someone in danger who refuses all help: a district psychiatrist can issue an examination order (הוראת בדיקה) under the Treatment of Mentally Ill Patients Law. Route the caller to the district health office or to the police, not to a chat line

**What the person beside them should do while waiting:** stay with them and do not leave them
alone, remove the means (medicines, weapons, car keys), and keep talking. Asking directly about
suicide does not plant the idea; it is the single most useful question available to a bystander.


## Patient rights during emergencies

Under Israeli law (Patient Rights Law, חוק זכויות החולה), patients have the following rights even during emergencies:

- Right to emergency care: no hospital or MDA crew may refuse emergency treatment regardless of payment status
- Informed consent (הסכמה מדעת): the patient must be told the procedure, its risks and the alternatives, except when unconscious
- Right to refuse treatment: a competent adult may refuse even in an emergency, with documented informed refusal
- Privacy (פרטיות), accompaniment by a family member unless medically contraindicated, and access to one's own medical records (רשומה רפואית)
- Language access: hospitals must provide interpretation for patients who do not speak Hebrew

Important: If a patient is unconscious or unable to give consent, medical staff may perform life-saving procedures under the emergency exception (pikuach nefesh, פיקוח נפש).
