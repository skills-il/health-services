# Bituach Leumi disabled-child claim for food allergy

Source of every criterion below: the Bituach Leumi allergy criterion page,
https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/Disabled_Child/likuilist/Pages/allergy.aspx

## The gate, in the order to check it

Check age first. It disqualifies more families than everything else combined.

| Element | Criterion, as published |
|---|---|
| Age window | From age 9 months to age 10 years |
| Rate | 50%, amounting to 1,943 NIS |
| Diagnostic confirmation | Allergy test criteria: skin prick test (SPT), blood test, or challenge test (מבחן תגר) |
| Allergist recommendation | Recommendation from a physician specialising in allergy to be permanently equipped with an EpiPen, or a reasoned justification for refraining from carrying one. Also qualifying alternative 5, see below |
| Episode documentation | Real-time medical documentation of the treatment given during an attack that included a respiratory component or anaphylactic shock |

## The five qualifying alternatives, only one of which is needed

Bituach Leumi's wording is "הזכאות לקצבה היא למי שעונה על תנאי הזכאות, ועל אחד
מהתנאים האלה", followed by five bullets. The child underwent a food allergy event
characterised by (1) anaphylactic shock, or (2) an anaphylactic reaction meaning a
clinical response of two different body systems, or (3) shortness of breath
characterised by a drop in oxygen saturation, or (4) treatment with injected
adrenaline; **or (5) there is a standing recommendation from an allergy specialist
to be permanently equipped with an EpiPen (or a reasoned justification for
refraining).**

**Alternative 5 is the correction made in v1.1.0.** Until then this skill listed
four alternatives and treated the allergist recommendation only as a supporting
document. It is both: a required document AND a qualifying route in its own right.
The distinction decides real claims, because a child with a diagnosed
life-threatening allergy will normally have the recommendation and may have no
documented episode at all. Check 5 before concluding a family does not qualify.

The rate updates every January along with the rest of the Bituach Leumi
schedule. Always restate the effective year and send the family to the source
before they budget on the figure.

## Why the episode documentation is the element that fails

Note first that alternative 5 sidesteps this entirely. What follows applies when
the family is qualifying on one of the four event routes.

The tests and the allergist letter are static. A family that has an allergist has the test results
and can request the EpiPen recommendation letter at any routine appointment.

The third cannot be produced on demand. It has to be captured at the moment of
an actual reaction, in the emergency room or clinic, at the visit itself. A
family that has had two frightening anaphylactic episodes and never collected
the paperwork has no admissible evidence of either.

**The forward-looking action.** At the first conversation with any newly
diagnosed family, tell them: if the child is ever treated for a reaction
involving breathing difficulty or anaphylaxis, ask for the treatment
documentation before leaving. Keep it. It costs nothing at the time and it is
the difference between a claim that succeeds and one that does not.

**The second contemporaneous record.** Where the reaction happened at a school,
gan or tzaharon and required emergency evacuation or use of the injector, the
institution must file it as an exceptional incident (0042 section 6.11), and a
tzaharon reports to the local authority's education department or the operator.
That record is made at the time by a third party. Tell families to request a
copy: it corroborates the medical documentation and survives the case where the
family left the ER empty-handed.

## Note on the rate

50% is the rate published for the allergy criterion specifically. The
disabled-child allowance carries different rates under different criteria, and
the sibling `israeli-oncology-navigator` skill documents a four-rate structure
for other conditions. Never carry a rate across from one criterion to another.

## If the child is over 10

Say so directly. A claim on the allergy criterion will not succeed. Two things
remain available and are worth redirecting to:

1. The Ministry of Education accommodations, which carry no age limit at all.
2. A different criterion on the same impairment list, which carries no shared age
   cap. The two that overlap with allergy most often are "בעיות בדרכי הנשימה
   וסיסטיק פיברוזיס (CF)", reachable on the same active-asthma documentation that
   serves as the fallback here, and "קצבה לילד עם מספר ליקויים" where several
   conditions combine. Send the family to the impairment list rather than closing
   the door; the claim itself belongs with the general BTL process.
