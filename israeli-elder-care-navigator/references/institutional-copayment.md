# Institutional Care Co-Payment (Hishtatfut Atzmit) -- How the Family's Share Is Computed

Governing document: **חוזר מנכ"ל משרד הבריאות 08/2018, "סיוע במימון האשפוז הסיעודי הכרוני"**.
Circular date 21.03.2018, in force from 08.04.2018, last updated 17.04.2023. It replaced circular 24/2005.
As of the Ministry of Health service page's 31.03.2026 update, 08/2018 is still the operative circular; no newer one exists.

- Landing page: https://www.gov.il/he/pages/mk08-2018
- Full PDF: https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/mk08-2018/he/files_circulars_mk_MK08_2018.pdf
- Official calculator: https://me.health.gov.il/nursing-calculator/

**Every shekel outcome here depends on two constants that change annually and are deliberately NOT published inside the circular: the income-tax credit point (נקודת זיכוי, s.33א of the Income Tax Ordinance) and the single old-age pension. Teach the computation, then send the family to the official calculator and to the district health office treasury (גזברות לשכת הבריאות) for the actual number. Do not quote a figure the circular does not contain.**

## Which track: code (MoH) or complex nursing (kupat cholim)

This file covers the Ministry of Health "code" route for siudi and tashush nefesh patients. A
**complex-nursing patient (siudi murkav)** is a different route (Kol Zchut, "סידור מוסדי עבור חולים
סיעודיים מורכבים"), under the State Health Insurance Law:

- Arranged through the **kupat cholim**. In hospital: the ward social worker refers to the kupa
  liaison nurse, who asks a kupa geriatrician to approve. From home: the family doctor refers to
  the kupa geriatrician in charge of complex-nursing placements.
- After approval the patient picks an institution from the list, by vacancy; once payment is
  arranged the kupa issues a commitment (hitchayvut).
- **The kupa may not refuse placement only because the co-payment is not yet arranged**, but
  unpaid co-payment is later collected.
- The co-payment is set by the kupa, varies by kupa and insurance type, and changes from time to
  time. Kol Zchut does not describe it as computed under circular 08/2018; the children's income
  is looked at on appeal. Ask the kupa for the figure rather than running the table below.
- Up to 80% of the old-age pension and/or income supplement can be diverted to the kupa.
- Appeal: an appeal form with the patient's, spouse's and children's income documents, filed at
  the kupa home clinic; then the kupa's member-rights department.
- A complex-nursing patient who goes on leave or into a general hospital can lose the place and
  needs a new form 17 to be readmitted.

## Who pays, and in what order (s.1)

Three payer groups are assessed: the patient, their spouse, and every adult child (each child is a "מתחייב"). Children's participation applies from age 21. A child already charged for another parent may be exempt.

**Sources are drawn in this order, each only if the earlier ones fall short of the full actual cost (s.1):**

1. Current income of the patient and spouse (s.1.1). All of it goes to the co-payment, apart from the listed exclusions, the pocket money and what a spouse at home retains (s.2.1.1).
2. Current income from the patient's or spouse's real estate (s.1.2).
3. **Financial assets of the patient or spouse** (s.1.3), subject to the protections in s.2.3 below.
4. Current income of the adult children (s.1.4).

**A child living abroad is not charged (s.3.1.3).** Each other adult child gets a separate computation.

## The patient's own share

- Computed from current income and financial assets under section 2 of the circular.
- **Floor, s.2.1.3 (verbatim):** "השתתפותו העצמית של מתאשפז לא תפחת מ 37% מגובה קצבת זקנה ליחיד." The same 37% floor is restated in s.2.1.5.1 where the couple lives only on a couple's old-age pension plus income supplement (no benefit-splitting is performed in that case).
- **Pocket money (דמי כיס), s.2.1.2 and the definitions section:** defined only by reference. Verbatim: "סכום הנותר למטופל מקצבה המשולמת לו לפי חוק הביטוח הלאומי, בהתאם לחלוקת קצבה לפי סעיף 307 לחוק, ולפי תקנות הביטוח הלאומי (חלוקת קצבה) תשמ"ד-1984, או סכום אחר המושאר למטופל מתוך הכנסותיו, לפי קביעת ועדת ערר." **The Ministry of Health does not publish a shekel figure for it, but Bituach Leumi does:** when the old-age pension is split to the paying public body, the resident keeps 662 NIS a month, or 607 NIS with income supplement (from 01.01.2026, https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/old_age/Pages/HalukatKitzva.aspx). An appeal committee may set a different amount. Quote the BL figure with its date, never a figure from memory.

## What is left to a spouse who stays at home (s.2.1.5.2)

Out of the couple's current income, and to the extent it suffices, the spouse retains: the patient's pocket money; the couple's health tax; the spouse's own old-age pension if entitled; the value of 18 credit points; where both receive a couple's pension without income supplement, an amount equal to the income supplement for a single person; the full amount of any health or disability benefit paid to the spouse personally; rent or mortgage and municipal tax on the home; the spouse's supplementary-health premium; home help worth 3 credit points a month; half a credit point for telephone; plus 9 credit points for each additional dependent with no income of their own.

## The patient's savings: what is protected (s.2.3, s.2.4.1)

- **Last expenses, s.2.3.1(b):** savings worth up to 100 credit points are left to the patient for
  last expenses. If both spouses are in MoH care, each keeps that amount (s.2.4.1).
- **Joint savings with a spouse who stays at home, s.2.3.2:**
  - up to 350 credit points: the savings are NOT realised at all;
  - over 350 and up to 700 credit points: 350 credit points are left to the spouse at home and the
    rest counts as the patient's;
  - over 700 credit points: the savings are split in half; half stays with the spouse at home and
    half counts as the patient's.
- Savings held jointly with someone other than the spouse are treated as 100% the patient's unless
  the district health office legal adviser decides otherwise (s.2.3.3).
- A savings plan or provident fund maturing within 6 months of admission is not broken early; it
  is paid by a post-dated cheque. One maturing later must be redeemed (s.2.3.4).
- Translate credit points into shekels at the current credit-point value; do not quote a shekel
  figure from memory.

## The patient's home: when it counts (s.2.2, s.2.9.2)

- **s.2.2.1: real estate counts only where no spouse, and no dependant of the patient or spouse,
  lives in it.** A spouse staying in the family flat means its rent or rental value is NOT added.
- A non-dependent relative living in the flat, paying or not, counts as a third-party tenant
  (footnote to s.2.2.3), so rent is charged from the date of the code.
- If the property was already let at the time of admission, the rent is charged as part of the co-payment from the date the code is issued (s.2.2.3).
- If it was not let, the patient is charged the rent, or the assessed rental value, as part of the co-payment from two months after admission or from the day letting actually begins, whichever is earlier (s.2.2.4).
- Only if the patient has no family (ariri) or is under public guardianship so letting is not possible, **a charge (הערת שיעבוד) is registered against the property at the Land Registry in favour of the Ministry of Health, at the level of six months' full hospitalisation cost** (s.2.2.5, verbatim: "תירשם הערת שיעבוד אצל רשם המקרקעין על הנכס, לטובת משרד הבריאות, בגובה מלוא עלות האשפוז במשך 6 חודשים").
- Running costs on the property (arnona, water, electricity, telephone, building committee) are NOT offset against the rent (s.2.2.6).
- Where a **public guardian who is not a family member** is appointed (s.2.9), the eligibility assessment may be deferred, at the guardian's request, up to 3 months from the appointment for an "organisation period", but that deferral does **not** relieve anyone of paying for the period, and a charge for three months' full cost is registered against any property the patient owns (s.2.9.2, s.2.9.2.2).

- **A flat given to a relative without consideration less than five years before the application**
  makes the placement conditional on that relative undertaking to pay its rental value (s.2.2.10).

Tell families this before they file, in both directions: a flat with a spouse still living in it
is not charged, and an empty flat is.

## The children's share -- a credit-point lookup, NOT a percent of income

This is the part families get wrong. There is no participation percentage. Section 3.5.1 defines a four-step lookup:

1. **s.3.5.1.1** Take the child's gross monthly average income (last three months), **after** the housing deduction of s.3.4, and divide by the **number of persons fixed by s.3.2** (table below). This gives "הכנסה ממוצעת ברוטו לנפש". **It is not a head count:** a single son with no children is divided by 3, not by 1, and dividing by 1 overstates his charge several times over.
2. **s.3.5.1.2** Divide that per-capita figure by the value of ONE credit point, expressing the income in credit points.
3. **s.3.5.1.3** Look the result up in **טבלת חיוב ילדי המטופל** (below): column ב is per-capita income in credit points, column ג is the number of credit points that child must pay. Multiply column ג by the value of one credit point.
4. **s.3.5.1.4 (verbatim):** "הסכום שהתקבל בסעיף 3.5.1.3 יוכפל ב-125% והתוצאה תהא סכום ההשתתפות העצמית שעל המתחייב לשלם."

### Number of persons (s.3.2), a fixed scale

| Child's (payer's) family status | 0 children | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No spouse | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
| Spouse with no income from any source | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
| Spouse with income | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |

- Children counted: each child under 21 of the payer **or of the payer's spouse** living with them, and each child the payer must
  maintain under a court judgment (s.3.2.3). For a maintained child living elsewhere the circular
  allows the alternative that gives the LOWER charge: count the child, or deduct the maintenance
  plus the payer's marginal tax rate from income.
- **A child of the payer aged 21 to 26 counts as 1/4 person, even if not living with them (s.3.2.4).**

### Income counted (s.3.3)

- Employee: all personal current gross income EXCEPT travel allowance, car and telephone expense
  refunds, meal-expense refunds, tax gross-up, recuperation pay, clothing allowance, and one-off
  payments such as back pay, a jubilee grant or a 13th salary (s.3.3.1).
- Self-employed: the last tax assessment as determined by the Tax Authority, index-linked and
  divided into a monthly figure (s.3.3.2).
- A capitalised pension counts at its gross amount before capitalisation (s.3.3.3).
- Rent from a property the child owns counts, but the mortgage on that same property may be
  offset (s.3.3.4).

### Housing deduction (s.3.4)

| Child's situation | Deduct from income |
|---|---|
| No spouse, or a spouse with no income | The FULL monthly mortgage repayment or rent, plus their marginal tax rate |
| Spouse has income from any source | HALF the monthly mortgage repayment or rent, plus their marginal tax rate |

### Not counted as the child's income (s.3.3.5)

The spouse's income and financial assets; child allowances and disabled-child benefit; any benefit paid to the child or their child personally because of health, disability or functional limitation (disability pensions, attendance allowance, mobility, work-injury disability, hostile-action victims, Defence Ministry disability, ringworm and polio compensation); the monthly allowance and annual grant under the Holocaust Survivors' Benefits Law that s.8A excludes; other income a statute says is not income; private insurance payouts; the child's own savings and financial assets.

## Refusing to sign (s.5.3)

A family member who refuses to sign the undertaking is charged the whole gap between the other
family members' payments and the institution's full actual cost, and collection proceeds against
them.

## Caps and special tracks

- **Family cap, s.3.5.2 (verbatim):** "סכום ההשתתפות העצמית של המטופל, בן/בת זוגו וכל המתחייבים ('ההשתתפות העצמית המשפחתית') לא יעלה על מלוא עלות האשפוז בפועל של אותו מטופל." If the family total exceeds the actual cost, each CHILD's share is reduced pro-rata until the total equals the full cost.
- **Both parents institutionalised, s.3.6.1:** where both of a child's parents were institutionalised within 12 months, that child's participation is computed and charged ONCE only. The family total for the two patients may not exceed the full actual cost of both.
- **Spouse in a welfare-funded institution, s.2.4.4:** the Ministry of Health runs the normal eligibility test but collects only **60%** of the family co-payment it determines; under the inter-ministry arrangement the remaining 40% falls to the Ministry of Welfare side. Section 2.4.5 applies the same 60% where both spouses were already in a welfare-funded institution.
- **A child who is a kibbutz or moshav shitufi member, s.3.6.2:** their income is the kibbutz's published "income per working person", and their personal kibbutz allowance is not counted.
- **Kibbutz or moshav shitufi member, s.2.5.1:** where the kibbutz has not been privatised or has a contractual obligation to its veteran members, the kibbutz pays **40% of the full actual hospitalisation cost, with NO means test** on the patient, spouse or children, and no benefit-splitting is performed. Section 2.5.2: where the kibbutz has no such obligation, the ordinary assessment runs.

## There is no national tariff

"מלוא עלות האשפוז בפועל" is defined verbatim as: "הסכום שמשלם משרד הבריאות עבור האשפוז, לחודש, במוסד בו מאושפז המטופל, ומתעדכן לפי ההסכמים בין משרד הבריאות למוסדות סיעודיים."

The cost is therefore **per institution**, set by the Ministry's agreements with each nursing institution and updated under them. There is no single published national monthly rate. Ask the district health office treasury, or ask to see the figure applied to the specific institution. Never quote a national monthly figure.
