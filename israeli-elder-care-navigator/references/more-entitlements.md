# Further Entitlements, Process Rules and Support Services

Read from the primary pages on 2026-10-01. Body steps point here. Each figure has an evidence
entry; re-read the page before quoting a figure in a new year.

## Gimlat siud: assessment, re-examination and appeal

Source: https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/Long_Term_Care/Pages/adltest.aspx and
https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/Long_Term_Care/Pages/reevaluate.aspx

- **The assessment is mostly made from the documents you send.** Attach an up-to-date medical
  summary signed by a treating or specialist physician, hospital discharge summaries, test and
  imaging results, and the medication list. If the assessor lacks detail, they phone the claimant
  and the family contact and complete the picture by phone or by a home visit. Ask for a visit in
  that call if you want one. In some cases eligibility is set on the medical documents alone.
- **Lives alone:** someone who lives alone, or lives with a family member who is also dependent,
  gets extra points in the dependency test as "boded". Say so on the claim.
- **Age 90+:** the claimant may choose to be assessed by a geriatric specialist instead of a BL
  assessor, at a hospital or public clinic. The geriatrician may not charge for it (not through
  supplementary insurance and not privately, including sharap).
- **Age 90+ with 6 points:** the benefit is set at level 4 instead of level 3, automatically.
- **Temporary eligibility:** an award can be for a fixed period. Under age 80, BL writes about two
  months before it ends; send the re-examination request about a month before the end.
- **Re-examination after deterioration:** the level can go up or stay the same but is never
  reduced; a higher level starts from the eighth day after the request. Level 6 recipients cannot
  ask for re-examination.
- **Appeal:** to an advisory committee (a physician and a nurse who are not BL employees) through
  the branch that handled the claim, **within 60 days** of the decision letter, or to the Labour
  Court. The level can rise or stay, but is not reduced as a result of the appeal. State whether
  you want the hearing in your presence.

## Around the claim: rehabilitation and bridging care

- **Rehabilitation comes first after a stroke or fall** (Kol Zchut, "שיקום רפואי לקשישים"). An
  elder whose motor or cognitive function was damaged by illness or injury is entitled to medical
  rehabilitation. It is the kupat cholim's responsibility, given on a physician's recommendation
  (in hospital, in the discharge letter). The hospital passes its recommendation to the kupa at
  least 2 working days before discharge, and a kupa geriatrics or rehab specialist must answer
  within 2 working days; any departure needs a reason. Inpatient rehab, where needed (for example
  after a severe stroke), lasts at least 25 days for neurological and 14 days for orthopaedic
  rehabilitation unless a shorter stay is justified in the records.
- **Whether the patient is "bar shikum" is a medical decision.** Inpatient rehab can last up to 3
  months, longer with approval of a kupa professional committee. Stroke and other neurological
  patients are referred only if a functional test shows their function has worsened significantly
  (Kol Zchut guide for an adult who became dependent).
- **Rehab and the benefit:** gimlat siud is not paid while the person stays in a nursing,
  complex-nursing, dementia or long-term ventilation ward, or in a rehabilitation setting under
  MoH supervision (BTL eligibility page). Time the caregiver hire to the rehab decision.
- **Trom siud (bridging care):** someone who filed a claim and needs care immediately gets 6
  weekly hours free from a siud company contracted with BL, until BL decides, for at most a month.
  The list comes from the social worker in the hospital, the kupa clinic, the municipal social
  services or the BL branch; a family can also approach a company directly.

## Who files, and how

Source: the Bituach Leumi long-term care "how to file" page, linked from
https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/Long_Term_Care/Pages/default.aspx

- From retirement age only.
- **Assisted filing by phone:** the senior-citizen volunteer advisers on **\*9696** fill in the
  claim with you and help send the documents.
- **In hospital:** a patient can file under the "Mahlaka Rishona" track through the hospital social
  worker. Someone hospitalised, or due to be within the month, can file immediately with an
  interim medical summary (sikum beinayim) from the hospital.
- **Living in a beit avot or an institution:** the management fills in section 9 of the claim form.

## Benefits that cannot be stacked with gimlat siud

Source: https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/Long_Term_Care/Pages/zakaut.aspx

A claimant who receives any of these must **choose** between it and gimlat siud: the attendance
allowance for the severely disabled (kitzvat sherutim meyuchadim), a personal-care or supervision
allowance from the State Treasury, or a Ministry of Defence ezrat hazulat or escort benefit.

### Attendance allowance after retirement age

Source: the Bituach Leumi attendance-allowance eligibility and rates pages, linked from
https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/Attendance_Allowance/Pages/default.aspx

- Someone entitled to the attendance allowance on the day they reach retirement age **keeps
  receiving it** after retirement age while they meet the conditions.
- A **first** claim may be filed up to **half a year after retirement age**. After that window the
  route is gimlat siud only. A parent who became severely dependent shortly before retirement age
  must claim inside that window.
- Rates from 01.01.2026: 50% = 1,943 NIS, 112% = 4,501 NIS, 188% = 7,181 NIS, 235% = 9,126 NIS.
- Compare the two before choosing: the attendance allowance is cash, while gimlat siud is mostly
  service hours. Someone staying in an institution that gives medical, nursing or rehabilitation
  services is not entitled to the attendance allowance (Kol Zchut guide).

## Holocaust survivors: extra long-term care

Source: https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/Long_Term_Care/Pages/nitzoleShoaa.aspx

Paid by the Holocaust Survivors' Rights Authority or the Claims Conference **on top of** the BL
benefit:

| Dependency points and benefit | Supplement |
|---|---|
| 6+ points, full benefit, care plan includes personal care hours at home | 9 extra weekly care hours |
| 6+ points, full benefit, care plan has other services and no home-care hours | Fixed monthly cash to the bank account |
| 6 points (BTL's wording; Kol Zchut says "at least 6"), benefit reduced because of income, where income exceeds the full-benefit ceiling by no more than 2,000 NIS a month | Monthly cash equal to 4.5 weekly hours, at 248 NIS per hour (from 01.08.2026) |
| 5 or 5.5 points, full benefit | Fixed monthly cash (amount not stated on the BTL page) |
| 1.5 or 2 points, so NO gimlat siud at all | Monthly cash equal to 2 weekly hours, at 248 NIS per hour (from 01.08.2026), subject to an income test |

**Read the 2,000 NIS row correctly.** The 2,000 NIS limits how far the survivor's income is above
the full-benefit ceiling (13,769 NIS for an individual filing April to December 2026), not the size
of the benefit. A survivor whose income is more than 2,000 NIS over that ceiling does not qualify for this row. BTL's page is
worded ambiguously; Kol Zchut states the income reading.

**The 1.5 to 2 point row is the one families miss.** A survivor told "not eligible for gimlat siud"
because they scored 1.5 or 2 points can still get this cash. Ask the Claims Conference
(03-5194400) or the Fund for the Welfare of Holocaust Victims.

BTL prints no shekel figure for the cash rows. Kol Zchut's figures are computed at an earlier hourly
rate, so do not quote them; confirm the current amount with the Fund.

**Two further routes to the 9 hours** (Kol Zchut), each subject to an income and asset declaration:
attendance-allowance recipients at 112% or more, and recipients of Ministry of Defence ezrat hazulat
of at least 66 monthly hours.

**The extra hours are given only at home.** They are not given to someone in a nursing institution,
a beit avot, or a frail or nursing ward of a diur mugan.

**Short-term care after a hospital stay.** A survivor who receives no gimlat siud, attendance
allowance or ezrat hazulat, with monthly gross income (excluding survivor payments) up to 11,480 NIS,
may get up to 50 care hours for up to two months, or until gimlat siud starts, whichever is
earlier. After a hospital stay it is requested **only through the hospital social worker, during
the stay**. A health crisis at home needs a recommendation from a physician and a social worker.
At most 4 requests a year, 3 months apart.

Other survivor payments (Step 6d): Claims Conference Article 2 Fund monthly pension; the
Israeli supplement from the Holocaust Survivors' Rights Authority; and the Claims Conference annual
payment of 1,350 euro in 2026 (1,400 euro in 2027) for survivors recognised for the Hardship Fund
one-time grant who receive no monthly persecution pension.

## Certificate, arnona and travel benefits

- **Gimlat siud certificate** (https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/Long_Term_Care/Pages/TeudatZcayLgimlatSioud.aspx):
  levels 1 to 3 get free entry for an escort at public places; levels 4 to 6 also get a queue
  exemption. BL does not know which venues honour it.
- **Arnona:** the Arnona Discount Regulations allow a discount of **up to 70%** for a gimlat siud
  recipient. The municipality sets the actual rate, so apply to the municipality.
- **Public transport:** free from 67 with a Rav-Kav on the senior profile (since 25.04.2025);
  women aged 62 to 67 get 50%.

## Pocket money when the old-age pension goes to an institution

Source: https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/old_age/Pages/HalukatKitzva.aspx

When a resident's old-age pension is split and paid to the public body funding the placement, the
resident keeps pocket money of **662 NIS** a month, or **607 NIS** for a recipient of income
supplement (from 01.01.2026); the rest goes to the paying body, and part may go to a spouse at
home. If both spouses are in an institution, both receive pocket money.

## Legal capacity: the less restrictive routes

Source: the Legal Capacity and Guardianship Law, 5722-1962, as amended in 2016.

- **Least restrictive first (s.33A):** a court appoints a guardian only where the purpose cannot
  be achieved in a way that restricts the person's rights, liberty and independence less, after
  examining alternatives including a decision-making supporter. It does not appoint a guardian
  for someone who cannot manage their affairs but can make decisions about them, save in special
  circumstances.
- **Supported decision-making (tomech bekabalat hachlatot, s.67B):** the court may appoint a
  supporter for an adult who can make decisions with support. The supporter helps gather
  information and explain options but does not decide in the person's name.
- **Advance directives for appointing a guardian (s.35A):** a competent person can state in
  advance whom they want as guardian. Signed before a lawyer on the prescribed form and
  deposited with the Administrator General; deposit is a condition of validity.
- **Expression-of-wishes document (s.64A):** a guardian of a relative (including a de facto
  guardian) can name, in a document signed before a lawyer, whom they want appointed as that
  relative's guardian if they themselves can no longer care for them. Relevant to
  an elder who is the guardian of a disabled adult child, not to the elder's own planning.
- **Who signs an enduring POA:** the enduring POA is made and signed before a lawyer trained by the
  Administrator General, but a **medical-only** enduring POA may be signed before a professional
  instead (physician, psychologist, nurse or social worker). Useful in hospital, where such a
  professional may be reachable when a lawyer is not.

## Equipment, emergency and volunteer support

- **Yad Sarah** (\*6444 or 02-6444444), branches nationwide, no income test: medical equipment loans
  (walkers, wheelchairs, oxygen, hospital beds, bath chairs), free or against a nominal deposit;
  a monitored emergency button from about 18 NIS a month; loneliness-prevention visits.
- **Ezer Mizion:** free or low-cost transport to medical appointments; book ahead, ideally days.
- **Eshel - JDC Israel:** community elder care programmes and supportive community.
- **Russian-speaking elders:** many BL, kupat cholim and MoH documents exist in Russian, and BL's
  senior line \*9696 is the first call for help in filing.
