## Instructions

### Step 1: Israel's Fertility Treatment Landscape

The governing law is the National Health Insurance Law (חוק ביטוח בריאות ממלכתי), and an annual committee (ועדת סל בריאות) decides what enters the basket. Treatment happens in licensed fertility units, mostly in public hospitals.

In the basic basket: IVF and ICSI (up to 2 living children), IUI and ovulation induction, medically indicated egg freezing, the insemination (not the vial) in sperm donation, egg donation minus a statutory fee, and PGT for listed indications. Elective egg freezing is not in the basket (Step 5).

### Step 2: Health Basket Coverage for Fertility

The health basket (sal briut, סל בריאות) defines what is covered under the National Health Insurance Law.

| Coverage Rule | Details |
|---------------|---------|
| Eligibility | All Israeli residents registered with a kupat cholim |
| Live birth limit | Funding runs until 2 living children from the current relationship. The statute counts children, not pregnancies. Twins from one funded birth exhaust it: `במקרה שנולדו תאומים, תיחשב האישה כמי שמיצתה את זכותה` |
| Age, own eggs | 18 to 45, `עד יום הולדת 45`. A commitment issued at 44 must be used before the official 45th birthday |
| Age, donated egg | Funded treatment with a donated egg runs until the 54th birthday. Turning 45 ends the own-egg track, not fertility coverage as a whole |
| Embryos frozen before 45 | Age 45 limits new retrievals, not transfers. Maccabi's published terms fund thawing and transferring an embryo until the 54th birthday if stimulation and retrieval were done before the 45th; confirm your kupah's terms |
| Other eligible groups | A woman without children having a child with a man who is not her partner (shared parenting, after a detailed agreement between them), patients in PGD for a couple at high risk of a genetic disease, and intended parents in an Israeli surrogacy process (the IVF stages, not the surrogacy costs) |
| Cycle rule from 42 | Per MoH Circular 6/2014, a woman who has turned 42 may not perform more than 3 consecutive IVF cycles that did not reach embryo transfer. Any transfer, even in the third cycle, restarts the count |
| Mandatory team review | After 4 consecutive cycles with no transfer, or 8 IVF cycles with no clinical pregnancy, the treating team (with a social worker) must review the causes and the plan going forward |
| Single women and female couples | Single women (em yechidanit) get the same IVF and donor-insemination coverage as couples, and each partner in a female couple qualifies in her own right. For a child born from anonymous sperm donation, the non-biological mother is recognised as a parent through a judicial parenthood order (tzav horut psikati) from the Family Court, without an adoption process |
| Reciprocal IVF | NOT permitted in Israel. One partner's egg may be fertilized here, but not transferred into her partner's uterus in Israel |
| Relationship change | For a couple, children from a previous marriage do not count: the entitlement is to a common child. The single-parent track is published for a woman without children, so a single woman who already has children should confirm with her kupah |
| Which kupah pays | The kupah where the WOMAN is insured funds treatment, even when the infertility is male-factor. Only treatment of the man's sperm is funded by his own kupah |

What the basket covers in each IVF cycle: monitoring ultrasounds and blood tests, egg retrieval with anesthesia, laboratory fertilization, embryo transfer, embryo freezing (5 years free, 5 more for a fee), and frozen embryo transfer (FET). Most kupot split IVF into 3 stages, each with its own commitment and copay: A, stimulation and tests; B, retrieval, fertilization and fresh transfer; C, thawing and transferring a frozen embryo. Fertility drugs are bought separately at the pharmacy (Maccabi caps the copay on injectable FSH and LH drugs at 243 NIS a month per treatment cycle, from 1.4.25). At Maccabi, IVF at a contracted public hospital without choice of doctor carries no charge beyond a quarterly 41 NIS clinic copay; a contracted private hospital with a chosen doctor is a SHABAN benefit (Maccabi Sheli, Zahav, Kesef) with per-stage copays. Ask your own kupah for its stage copays and drug cap. A donor sperm vial is NOT covered; the patient buys it herself.

The Health Basket Committee meets annually. The 2026 basket (Director General Circular 02/2026, published 08.03.2026) added PGT (preimplantation genetic testing) as part of IVF for carriers of high-cancer-risk mutations (BRCA1/BRCA2 and Lynch syndrome). That entitlement is capped: `הזכאות לטיפול לפי פסקה זו, תוגבל לשני הריונות בלבד, שהסתיימו בלידה`.

### Step 3: The IVF Process Through Kupat Cholim

Route: gynecologist or family doctor referral, then a fertility specialist (רופא פוריות), then approval by the kupah's fertility committee (ועדת פוריות), then a Form 17 commitment (טופס 17) to a licensed IVF unit in a public hospital. Diagnostics usually include a hormonal blood panel, ultrasound, semen analysis and HSG. `references/ivf-process-detailed.md` has the full walkthrough.

Choosing a clinic: verify the unit is in your kupah's agreements; wait times differ. Donor insemination may NOT be performed in a private clinic (`חל איסור לבצע הזרעה מלאכותית מתורם במרפאה פרטית`).

Safety note (OHSS, גירוי יתר שחלתי): ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome is the most common serious complication of stimulation. Severe abdominal pain or swelling, rapid weight gain, vomiting, breathlessness or a sharp drop in urine output after retrieval are emergency symptoms. Contact the treating fertility unit immediately, and go to an emergency room (or call 101) if severe.

### Step 4: Supplementary Insurance (SHABAN) for Additional Coverage

Supplementary health insurance (shaban, שב"ן, ביטוח משלים) extends fertility coverage beyond the basic health basket.

| Kupat Cholim | SHABAN Plan Names | Fertility Benefits |
|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Clalit (כללית) | Mushlam, Mushlam Platinum | Extra IVF cycles, elective egg freezing, choice of doctor |
| Maccabi (מכבי) | Maccabi Sheli, Zahav | Extended coverage, elective egg freezing, premium clinics |
| Meuhedet (מאוחדת) | Adif, Si (שיא) | Extra treatments, elective egg freezing, specialist access |
| Leumit (לאומית) | Kesef, Zahav | Fertility coverage, and since 1 July 2026 elective egg freezing on Zahav |

What SHABAN may add: extra IVF cycles for a third or later child, elective egg freezing (all four kupot, Step 5), choice of doctor, shorter waits, and certain costs around egg donation and treatment abroad.

Important: without SHABAN, treatment for a third child or beyond is fully out-of-pocket. SHABAN fertility benefits carry a qualification period (tkufat achshara, תקופת אכשרה), 12 months on the Clalit, Maccabi and Leumit elective egg-freezing benefits documented below, so enroll before you need it.

Switching kupah for a better fertility benefit: the move takes effect only on one of 6 fixed dates a year, set by when the form is filed. SHABAN seniority carries over only if you join a same-tier plan at the new kupah within 90 days of the switch; moving to a higher tier triggers its full qualification period. For elective egg freezing, Meuhedet Si and Leumit Zahav each credit 12 months of seniority in named SHABAN plans of other kupot, so the qualification period need not restart. No source says a Form 17 or committee approval carries over, so ask the new kupah for its own approval before the next cycle and time the switch between cycles.

### Step 5: Egg Freezing, Donation, Surrogacy, and Posthumous Use

**Egg Freezing (hakpaat bitziot, הקפאת ביציות):**

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Medical egg freezing | Basket-funded (eggs, embryos or ovarian tissue) for girls and women facing chemotherapy, radiation or ovary removal, and up to age 39 for women at increased risk of premature ovarian failure: diminished ovarian reserve, fragile-X premutation, a disease proven to raise that risk, gonadotoxic treatment not due to malignancy, planned removal of more than one ovary, or an ovarian endometrioma of at least 4 cm before surgery; also as part of gender transition. Toward a first and second child. The increased-risk and gender-transition tracks are capped at 4 cycles or 20 eggs (6 or 40 for fragile-X). Clalit states the oncology track runs to the 41st birthday |
| Elective egg freezing | NOT in the basic basket at any age, but permitted since 2011 for women who have turned 30 and not yet 41, at their own expense. Circular 1/2011 caps it at 6 retrievals, 25 eggs at ages 30 to 35 and 35 eggs at 36 to 40. These are Ministry of Health limits on a self-funded procedure, not private SHABAN limits. A woman who started before 41 may continue to the retrieval or egg cap while the process stays continuous |
| SHABAN subsidies (verify, these changed recently) | All at 3,500 NIS copay per cycle. Clalit Mushlam Platinum: 30 to 37 (`עד יום הולדת 38`), 2 retrievals / 25 eggs at 30 to 35, 3 retrievals / 35 eggs at 36 to 37. Maccabi Sheli: 31 to 38 inclusive, up to 3 treatments or 25 eggs. Meuhedet Si: no age band on its public page, so confirm with Meuhedet. Leumit Zahav: 30 to 37 inclusive, up to 4 rounds. Clalit, Maccabi and Leumit require 12 months of membership, and Clalit excludes women who already used a preservation entitlement |
| Storage term and cost | Eggs are kept up to 5 years. Without advance written notice that the woman wants storage extended, the unit may stop storing them 60 days after warning her, subject to MoH approval; non-payment triggers the same process. Self-funded freezing carries a storage fee (MoH tariff, taarifon). For MEDICAL preservation the basket funds storage until 2 children are born or the 42nd birthday, whichever comes first |
| Usage | The Ministry of Health lets women use their frozen eggs in fertility treatment until age 54. Whether the kupah funds thawing and fertilizing them after the 45th birthday is not stated in the sources, so ask before relying on it |

**Sperm Donation (trumat zera, תרומת זרע):**

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Legal status | Legal and regulated; donor insemination may not be done in a private clinic |
| Who can use | Single women, married couples with male infertility, same-sex female couples, and a single intended mother in a surrogacy process |
| Coverage | The kupah funds the medical treatment (insemination, monitoring, lab work). It does NOT fund the sperm: `את מנת הזרע האישה רוכשת על חשבונה`. A domestic vial costs several hundred shekels, an imported one can reach thousands, and several vials are often needed |
| Donor selection | Through licensed sperm banks (bank zera, בנק זרע). Donors in Israel are anonymous; typically origin, physical characteristics, education and blood type are shared. A donor may withdraw consent and block use of his vials as long as they have not yet been used |
| Imported sperm | The Israeli bank imports it, only from a foreign bank the Ministry of Health approved (list on gov.il). An imported donor may be anonymous or one willing to have his name given to the child at 18 |
| Pending reform | A public committee chaired by Prof. Shlomo Mor-Yosef (convened 2025) is examining an identified donation track. No change is in force yet |

**Egg Donation (trumat bitziot, תרומת ביציות):**

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Legal status | Legal and regulated under the Egg Donation Law 2010 (chok trumat bitziot, חוק תרומת ביציות) |
| Who can use | Israeli resident women from 18 until their 54th birthday with a medical opinion that they cannot conceive from their own eggs |
| Recipient fee | A statutory 10,000 NIS paid to the hospital, reduced to 7,000 NIS for a recipient on the income-support benefit (havtachat hachnasa) with Bituach Leumi confirmation. This fee also funds the donor's compensation. Beyond it the kupah funds the treatment as part of the basket |
| Matching | Same religion, not relatives, donor not married, unless an exceptions committee (vaadat charigim) approves |
| In surrogacy | A surrogate may never also be the egg donor. A single intended mother may not use a donated egg for surrogacy |
| Donation from abroad | Possible only through an Israeli IVF unit holding a Ministry of Health permit to work with units abroad, which organizes the procedure abroad or brings the fertilized eggs to Israel; the financing is arranged through that unit |

**Surrogacy (pundakaut, פונדקאות):** legal under the Embryo Carrying Agreements Law 1996, with at least one intended parent genetically linked to the child. Couples, a single intended mother and a single intended father are eligible, following the Supreme Court ruling in HCJ 781/15 (11.7.2021) and Director General Circular 7/2021 (27.12.2021). Intended parents must be Israeli residents, at least one under 54 at approval, with no more than 2 common (or, for a single parent, genetic) children; an intended mother needs a medical opinion that she cannot conceive and carry a pregnancy, or that pregnancy would seriously endanger her health. The committee will not approve eggs retrieved after age 43, and a single intended mother must use her own egg (no donated egg), so for her only eggs retrieved by 43 work. The surrogate must be an Israeli resident, at least 22 and not yet 39 at approval, who has given birth before and is not a relative. The intended parents' IVF stages are basket-funded; the surrogacy costs are not, and there is no official tariff. `references/surrogacy-law-guide.md` has the committee, the embryo-import rule, warnings about surrogacy abroad and the full process.

**Posthumous sperm use and embryo disputes:** there is no statute. The state acts under the 2003 Attorney General directive, use of the sperm always requires Family Court approval, and embryo disputes after separation turn on the Nahmani case. `references/posthumous-and-embryo-law.md` has retrieval timing, the wartime rule, who may apply, contacts, embryo storage terms and dispute rules; consult it for any posthumous-use or embryo-disposition question.

### Step 6: Male-Factor Infertility and Fertility Preservation

The workup is a semen analysis (bdikat zera), usually repeated, then a urologist or andrologist; ICSI is basket-funded when indicated. Azoospermia is investigated at the fertility unit, and surgical retrieval (TESE, micro-TESE) belongs to that pathway, so ask the unit and the kupah what is covered first. Oncology patients get kupah participation in sperm-storage costs, limited to 5 years. Raise preservation BEFORE chemotherapy or radiation starts; egg, embryo and ovarian tissue freezing are listed basket methods, including for girls and adolescents. For pre-pubertal boys, ask the oncology unit; do not assume coverage. `references/ivf-process-detailed.md` has the male-factor detail.

### Step 7: Work Rights and Bituach Leumi During Treatment

| Right | Details |
|-------|---------|
| Absence, women | Charged against accrued sick days: up to 4 treatment series a year of up to 16 days (5-day week, 64 a year) or 20 days (6-day week, 80 a year). State employees: 18 or 22 per series, up to 72 or 88 |
| Absence, men | 12 days a year against accrued sick days, for treatment he is undergoing |
| Partial days | Up to 40 hours a year as single hours, paid from the first hour |
| Sick pay, whole days | Day 1 unpaid, days 2 and 3 at 50%, full from day 4. For women this restarts each treatment series; for men it runs per year, and days beyond 12 are unpaid |
| Anti-dismissal | No dismissal or pay cut during the absence and for 150 days after, if you gave notice with a medical certificate, the treatment is toward at most 2 births, and fewer than 2 years have passed since the first absence. Even without absence, after 6 months with the employer, there is protection if you report the treatment within 3 working days of a dismissal notice. Only the person in treatment is protected, not the partner. Career soldiers (keva) have a parallel protection |
| Reserve duty (miluim) | A male reservist in treatment, or whose partner is, gets at least 5 days (clinic) or 10 days (IVF) on Form 58; surgical sperm retrieval adds 3 days before to 3 months after, for the man undergoing it. The male tracks do not apply to an emergency call-up (Tzav 8). A female reservist goes through her unit doctor, who decides case by case |
| Bituach Leumi | Treatment itself carries no benefit. Once pregnant, shmirat heriyon (שמירת היריון) covers a certified absence of at least 30 consecutive days (14 for a subsequent period); dmei leda does not depend on how the pregnancy was conceived |
| Bereaved families | Families of victims of hostile acts can get Bituach Leumi participation in fertility and surrogacy costs (up to 28,562 NIS in 2026) after kupah and SHABAN funding |

These rights do not depend on your kupah or SHABAN plan. `references/work-and-service-rights.md` has the conditions, the three reserve-duty tracks, keva rules and the bereavement track.

### Step 8: Costs and Emotional Support

| Item | Covered by Health Basket | Out-of-Pocket |
|------|------------------------|---------------|
| IVF or IUI cycle (first 2 living children) | Yes | Per-stage copays that differ by kupah and by public or private unit (Step 2) |
| IVF cycle (third child, no SHABAN) | No | Self-pay; get a written quote from the unit |
| Donor sperm vial | No | Several hundred NIS domestic, up to thousands imported |
| Fertility medications | Partly | Pharmacy copay, capped monthly at some kupot (Maccabi 243 NIS per treatment cycle for injectable FSH and LH) |
| PGT (BRCA/Lynch or an approved indication) | Yes, capped at 2 pregnancies ending in birth | Unapproved indications are self-pay |
| Egg freezing (medical indication) | Yes, per indication, storage until 2 children or the 42nd birthday | No copay at a public hospital at Maccabi; storage after the funded period |
| Egg freezing (elective) | No | 3,500 NIS per cycle on a subsidizing SHABAN plan, otherwise full price plus storage |
| Egg donation (recipient) | Yes | Statutory 10,000 NIS, or 7,000 NIS on income support |
| Surrogacy | The IVF stages only | Surrogacy costs are large and highly variable, no official tariff (Step 5) |

Support: every licensed fertility unit has a social worker, and kupot run free support groups. Amutat Chen (עמותת חן לפריון ולחיים), 03-5050345, offers counseling and peer groups. Kol HaBriut, the MoH information line, is *5400. For halachic guidance there are the Puah Institute, Bonei Olam and Keren Gefen, and Maccabi publishes an entitlement to a supervisor for fertility treatment (mashgicha, משגיחה על טיפולי פוריות).

## Examples

### Example 1: Starting IVF for the First Time
User says: "My wife and I have been trying with no success. How do we start IVF through our kupat cholim?"

Actions:
1. See your gynecologist or family doctor; a common referral threshold is 12 months of trying, or 6 if the woman is over 35
2. Get a referral to a fertility specialist; both partners are evaluated, and male-factor treatment of his sperm is funded by HIS kupah
3. Get the kupah fertility committee's approval and a commitment for each IVF stage
4. Ask for the stage copays and the drug copay, and compare a public unit with a private one

Result: The couple enters the pathway through their kupat cholim and knows what they will pay.

### Example 2: Coverage for a Third Child
User says: "We already have two children from IVF. We want a third. Is IVF still covered?"

Actions:
1. Basic coverage ends after 2 living children from the current relationship (children from a previous marriage do not count)
2. Check your SHABAN plan for third-child cycles and its qualification period
3. Otherwise get a written quote from the unit for a self-paid cycle

Result: The couple determines their SHABAN coverage and plans accordingly.

### Example 3: Single Woman Considering Egg Freezing
User says: "I'm 33 and single. I want to freeze my eggs. Is this covered?"

Actions:
1. Elective freezing is NOT in the basic basket. It is permitted from 30 until the 41st birthday at your own expense, within the Circular 1/2011 caps (Step 5)
2. Medical freezing IS funded with a documented indication, so check whether one applies
3. Age 33 falls inside the Clalit, Maccabi and Leumit SHABAN bands (confirm Meuhedet), all at 3,500 NIS per cycle, so compare cycle counts, egg caps and the 12-month qualification periods
4. Plan for the 5-year storage term and the written renewal notice it requires

Result: She compares her kupah's terms against the others, and knows the procedure is self-funded but subsidized.

### Example 4: Exploring Surrogacy in Israel
User says: "We're a couple considering surrogacy. Is it legal in Israel and how does it work?"

Actions:
1. It is legal under the Embryo Carrying Agreements Law 1996, with at least one intended parent genetically linked to the child. Couples and single intended parents are eligible since HCJ 781/15 (2021), as long as you are Israeli residents, one of you is under 54, and you have no more than 2 common children
2. You apply to the approval committee with the required forms and medical, legal and psychological opinions
3. The surrogate must be an Israeli resident, at least 22 and not yet 39, who has given birth before and is not a relative, and she can never be the egg donor
4. Your IVF stages are basket-funded, but the surrogacy costs are large and variable with no official tariff, so get written quotes and read the Ministry of Health warnings about surrogacy abroad
5. Consult a lawyer specializing in surrogacy law before signing

Result: The couple begins the application with realistic expectations about cost and timeline.

## Gotchas

- The limit is living children (2), not cycles, but there IS a cycle rule from 42 (Circular 6/2014, Step 2). Agents say "no cycle limit" flatly and get this wrong. Twins from one funded birth exhaust the whole entitlement.
- For a woman starting at 40, give the rules as one timeline: own-egg retrievals funded until her 45th birthday, the cycle rule from 42, elective freezing (self-pay) only if started before her 41st birthday, and the Clalit, Maccabi and Leumit SHABAN bands all end before 40, while Meuhedet publishes no age band.
- Age 45 limits new retrievals, not transfers: embryos made before 45 can be transferred later (Maccabi funds it until the 54th birthday), and MoH lets frozen eggs be used until 54. Do not send a 46-year-old with stored embryos straight to egg donation.
- "Covered" is not "free": IVF stages carry kupah copays, fertility drugs a pharmacy copay, the donor sperm vial is self-purchased, and egg-donation recipients pay a statutory 10,000 NIS fee (7,000 NIS on income support).
- The 25-egg and 35-egg caps on elective freezing are Ministry of Health limits (Circular 1/2011), not private SHABAN limits.
- Reciprocal IVF is illegal in Israel, which matters for female same-sex couples who assume otherwise.
- Reserve duty has three tracks: a male reservist in treatment, a male reservist whose partner is in treatment (the same 5 or 10 days, as an entitlement), and a female reservist, who goes through her unit doctor.
- The complaint route is not the kupah's own ombudsman but the Public Complaints Commissioner for the National Health Insurance Law at the Ministry of Health, which can determine the kupah owed the service and, in appropriate cases, get the privately paid cost reimbursed.
- Posthumous sperm retrieval follows the 2003 Attorney General directive plus a wartime rule letting bereaved parents request retrieval without a court application. Agents omit this entirely.

## Troubleshooting

### Error: "My kupat cholim says I've used up my IVF coverage but I don't have 2 living children"
Cause: Coverage is based on living children, not cycles, so it may be a counting or administrative error. Real rules can also be behind it: the twins rule, the age-42 consecutive-cycle rule, or a mandatory team review (Step 2).
Solution: Ask the kupah in writing which rule it is applying. If you believe the rule was applied wrongly, the route is a complaint with the Public Complaints Commissioner for the National Health Insurance Law at the Ministry of Health, attaching medical documents, correspondence and receipts. The commissioner can determine the kupah owed you the service, in appropriate cases the cost of a privately purchased service is reimbursed, and its position supports a claim in the Regional Labour Court.

### Error: "I was told I'm too old for IVF coverage at age 46"
Cause: The own-egg ceiling really is 45 (`עד יום הולדת 45`), but it limits new retrievals, and the staff member may have stopped there. Embryos already frozen from a retrieval before 45 can still be transferred (Maccabi funds this until the 54th birthday). Funded treatment with a DONATED egg runs until the 54th birthday for Israeli-resident women with a medical opinion that they cannot conceive from their own eggs.
Solution: If you have stored embryos, ask the kupah for a stage C (frozen-embryo transfer) commitment. Otherwise ask the fertility unit to open the egg-donation track: a written medical opinion, the official application form to the responsible physician, entry into the Ministry of Health database, and matching. Budget the statutory 10,000 NIS recipient fee (7,000 NIS on income support); the rest is funded by your kupah. If the kupah refuses the donation track itself, complain to the National Health Insurance commissioner.

### Error: "I don't understand the difference between basic coverage and SHABAN for fertility"
Cause: Basic basket coverage and SHABAN overlap confusingly on fertility.
Solution: Basic coverage is the legal entitlement: IVF for the first 2 living children (own eggs to 45, donated eggs to 54), IUI, ICSI, medically indicated egg freezing and the funded PGT indications, with stage and drug copays. It does NOT cover the donor sperm vial, elective freezing or a third child. SHABAN is optional paid insurance that may add third-child cycles, subsidized elective freezing, choice of doctor and shorter waits, after a qualification period. Ask your kupah for its current fertility chapter in writing.
