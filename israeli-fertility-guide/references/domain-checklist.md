# Domain Coverage Checklist: Israeli Fertility Treatment

Anchor list for reviewing this skill's completeness. Scope: navigating fertility
treatment in Israel (coverage, rights, law, costs, support), not clinical care.

## Must cover (core)

1. IVF in the health basket, basic eligibility. IVF is a basket service every kupat cholim must provide under the National Health Insurance Law 1994. Source: health.gov.il fertility pages; Clalit IVF rights page.
2. The 2-live-births rule. The basket funds IVF up to two children from the woman's current relationship or family unit. For a couple, children from previous marriages do not count (the entitlement is to a common child); the single-parent track is published for a woman without children, so a single woman with children must confirm with her kupah. Source: Kol Zchut IVF page (verified 2026-09-13).
3. Layered age ceiling: funded own-egg IVF runs from 18 until the 45th birthday (a commitment given at 44 must be realized before it), and funded treatment with a DONATED egg runs until the 54th birthday. Source: Kol Zchut IVF and egg-donation pages; Maccabi eligibility 2565.
4. Cycle rules. There is no general cap on attempts, but from age 42 no more than 3 CONSECUTIVE cycles that did not reach embryo transfer (any transfer resets the count), and a mandatory treating-team review after 4 consecutive no-transfer cycles or 8 cycles with no clinical pregnancy. Also: twins from one funded birth exhaust the whole entitlement, because the statute counts children not pregnancies. Source: MoH Circular 6/2014 via Kol Zchut IVF page.
5. IUI vs ICSI vs IVF distinguished, all basket-covered when indicated. Source: kupot eligibility pages.
6. PGT/PGD coverage. Funded with committee approval for defined indications; the 2026 basket added PGT as part of IVF for carriers of high-cancer-risk mutations (BRCA1/BRCA2, Lynch). Source: 2026 basket protocols; gov.il PGD page.
7. SHABAN supplementary fertility benefits (extra cycles beyond two children, choice of doctor, shorter waits, premium meds), with waiting periods. Source: each kupah's SHABAN pages.
8. Medical egg freezing / fertility preservation (eggs, embryos, ovarian tissue), basket-funded for oncology patients and women before ovary removal, and up to 39 for women at increased early-menopause risk: diminished ovarian reserve, fragile-X premutation, a proven disease, gonadotoxic treatment not due to malignancy, removal of more than one ovary, or a unilateral endometrioma of at least 4 cm before surgery; also within gender transition. Toward a first and second child; the increased-risk and gender-transition tracks are capped at 4 cycles or 20 eggs (6 or 40 for fragile-X), no cap is published for the oncology track. Funded STORAGE runs until 2 children are born or the 42nd birthday, whichever comes first. Self sperm banking: oncology patients get kupah participation in storage, limited to 5 years. Source: Maccabi eligibility 1834; Clalit fertility-preservation page; Kol Zchut egg freezing and sperm-deposit pages.
9. Elective / social egg freezing, NOT basket-funded (self-pay), permitted from age 30 until the 41st birthday. MoH Circular 1/2011 caps it at 6 retrievals, 25 eggs at 30-35 and 35 eggs at 36-40; these are MoH limits on a self-funded procedure, NOT private SHABAN limits. All four kupot now subsidize it through SHABAN at a 3,500 NIS copay, on different bands: Clalit Platinum 30-37, Maccabi Sheli 31-38, Meuhedet Si (no age band on its public page, confirm), Leumit Zahav 30-37. Eggs are stored up to 5 years, renewable only on advance written notice. Source: Kol Zchut egg-freezing page; each kupah's eligibility page; ynet on the Leumit benefit.
10. Sperm donation, eligibility (single women, couples with male-factor infertility, each partner in a female same-sex couple) and mandatory anonymity by law. CRITICAL: the kupah funds the procedure but the woman buys the sperm vial herself, several hundred shekels domestic and up to thousands imported. Donor insemination may not be done in a private clinic. A 2025 public committee (Mor-Yosef) is examining an identified-donation track. Source: Kol Zchut kabalat trumat zera.
11. Egg donation under the Egg Donation Law 2010, recipients from 18 until the 54th birthday, basket-funded apart from a statutory recipient fee of 10,000 NIS (7,000 NIS on income support) which also funds the donor's compensation. Reciprocal IVF (transferring one partner's fertilized egg into the other partner) is not permitted in Israel. Source: Kol Zchut kabalat trumat bitzit.
12. Surrogacy under the Embryo Carrying Agreements Law 1996. Gestational only, statutory approvals committee, surrogate must be an Israeli resident who has turned 22 and not yet 39 at approval, has given birth before, and is not related to the intended parents. Source: gov.il embryo-carrying service.
13. Surrogacy eligibility after HCJ 781/15 (11.7.2021) and DG Circular 7/2021 (27.12.2021): couples, a single intended mother and a single intended father; intended parents Israeli residents, at least one under 54 at approval, at most 2 common (or genetic) children; surrogate of the same religion; eggs retrieved up to 43; two intended fathers may not mix sperm in one fertilization. The intended parents' IVF stages are basket-funded. Embryos fertilized abroad: temporary MoH order for contracts signed by 1.8.2027; the July 2026 memorandum is not law. Source: gov.il embryo-carrying service page and the MoH letter of 21.7.2026; Maccabi eligibility 31336; Kol Zchut IVF.
14. Single mother by choice, IVF and sperm-donation eligibility without a partner. Source: kupot eligibility; Kol Zchut.
15. Lesbian co-mother parenthood, each partner may receive donor sperm and the non-biological mother's parenthood is established via a judicial parenthood order; the order is granted by the Family Court under the Attorney General's conditions, without adoption. Source: Kol Zchut judicial-parenthood-order page.
16. Fertility-treatment work leave under the Women's Employment Regulations (Absence for Fertility and IVF Treatments) 1990: women up to 16 days per series (5-day week) or 20 days (6-day week), max 4 series per year, so 64 or 80 days a year; state-employed women 18 or 22 per series, so 72 or 88; MEN 12 days a year; plus up to 40 partial-day hours a year paid from the first hour. Against sick days, with advance notice and medical confirmation. Source: Kol Zchut absence page; Women's Employment Law.
17. Anti-dismissal protection under the Women's Employment Law, no dismissal during fertility-treatment absence and for 150 days after, without a Ministry of Labor permit. Source: Kol Zchut; gov.il worker-rights page.
18. Cost picture, what is covered vs out-of-pocket (third child without SHABAN, private clinics, elective freezing, storage, surrogacy). Figures without an authoritative source must be labelled market estimates or omitted: there is no official surrogacy tariff, no current published egg-donor compensation figure, and storage fees and drug copays vary by unit and kupah. Source: kupot; Kol Zchut; MoH tariff (taarifon).
19. PGT entitlement caps. The 2026 basket PGT for BRCA/Lynch carriers is limited to two pregnancies ending in birth, as is the severe-monogenic-disease indication. Source: Director General Circular 02/2026, Annex B.
20. Posthumous sperm retrieval and use. 2003 Attorney General directive, spouse standing vs parental standing, Family Court approval for use, and the Iron Swords wartime rule allowing hospitals to approve retrieval at bereaved parents' request without a court application. Source: gov.il sperm-preserving-after-death.
21. Embryo storage and disposition. Public Health Regulations (IVF) 1987, 5 free years plus 5 paid, thaw and destroy absent instruction, and the Nahmani doctrine on disputes after separation. Source: Kol Zchut embryo freezing; DN 2401/95.
22. Male-factor infertility. Semen analysis, andrology referral, sperm washing (hashbachat zera), azoospermia workup and TESE, and the rule that the WOMAN's kupah funds the couple's treatment while treatment of the man's sperm is funded by his own kupah. Source: Kol Zchut IVF page.
23. OHSS as a safety topic, with a when-to-seek-urgent-care line, not a coverage topic.
24. Bituach Leumi interface. Fertility treatment itself carries no BL benefit, only sick days; shmirat heriyon requires at least 30 consecutive days (14 for a subsequent period); dmei leda eligibility does not depend on the method of conception. Source: Kol Zchut shmirat heriyon.
25. The complaints route. Nitzivut kvilot hatzibur for the National Health Insurance Law at MoH, not the kupah's own ombudsman, with enforcement and reimbursement powers and the Regional Labour Court as the fallback. Source: gov.il national-health-insurance-law-complaint-submission.
26. Emotional support, fertility-unit social workers, support organizations, kupah groups. Source: Kol Zchut; support NGOs.
27. Reserve-duty release, three tracks: a male reservist in treatment (at least 5 days clinic, 10 days IVF, 3 days before to 3 months after surgical sperm retrieval, Form 58); a male reservist whose partner is in treatment (the same 5 or 10 days as an entitlement); a female reservist (unit doctor decides deferral or freezing). Not on an emergency call-up (Tzav 8). Source: the three Kol Zchut reserve-duty pages (verified 2026-11-11).
28. Switching kupah for a fertility benefit: 6 fixed effective dates a year, SHABAN seniority kept at the same tier, qualification periods for new services and a full period when moving up a tier. Source: Kol Zchut kupah-switching page.
29. Posthumous-use legislation status: the Continuity Law bill did not pass in the 25th Knesset; the 26th Knesset was elected in October 2026 and no revival was found as of 2026-11-11. The 2003 AG directive remains the basis. Re-check each cycle. Source: ynet, 25th Knesset summary.
30. Age 45 limits new retrievals, not transfers. Embryos made before 45 can be transferred later (Maccabi funds stage C until the 54th birthday when stages A and B were done before 45), and MoH lets women use frozen eggs until 54. Source: Maccabi eligibility 2565; gov.il oocyte-cryopreservation.
31. Cost structure: IVF in 3 stages with separate commitments and copays; fertility drugs bought with a pharmacy copay (Maccabi cap 243 NIS a month per treatment cycle for injectable FSH/LH, from 1.4.25); public unit without choice of doctor vs. private unit with per-stage copays. Source: Kol Zchut IVF; Maccabi eligibility 2565.
32. Imported donor sperm via an Israeli bank from an MoH-approved foreign bank; may be from an identity-release donor (name to the child at 18). Imported donor eggs via an Israeli IVF unit holding an MoH permit. Source: gov.il receiving-donor-sperm and israel-abroad-ovum-recipient.
33. Other eligible groups in the basket: shared parenting with a man who is not the woman's partner (detailed agreement first), and PGD patients at high genetic risk. Source: Kol Zchut IVF.
34. Dismissal protection conditions (civilian: notice and certificate, up to 2 births, 2 years; non-absent workers after 6 months) and the parallel career-soldier (keva) protection. Source: Kol Zchut anti-dismissal pages (verified 2026-11-11).
35. Bituach Leumi participation in fertility and surrogacy costs for bereaved families of hostile-act victims (28,562 NIS in 2026, doubled for multiple loss, after kupah and SHABAN, claim within a year). Source: Kol Zchut bereaved-families page.

## Should cover (advanced)

1. Frozen embryo transfer (FET) as stage C (now covered, item 30/31); whether an FET counts as a cycle for Circular 6/2014 is unsourced.
2. SHABAN waiting periods and per-child caps (the volatile pieces, re-verify against live circulars).
3. Fertility preservation for cancer patients named explicitly as the largest medical-indication group.
4. Importing donor sperm or eggs from abroad (now covered, item 32).
5. Embryo disposition disputes on divorce or separation (Nahmani precedent and later case law).
6. Religious or halachic dimension, supervision of gamete handling and donor-religion selection.
7. Bituach Leumi interface, treatment funding is via the kupot and basket while maternity and related benefits sit with the National Insurance Institute.
8. Choice of fertility unit and wait-time differences between hospitals.
9. The pending identified-sperm-donation reform (Mor-Yosef committee) as a watch item.

## Out of scope (explicit)

Re-litigated 2026-11-11: every row below would still be asked by some users, and none became capturable as general information, so each stays out of scope with its referral. Career-soldier (keva) dismissal protection is now covered (item 34). Kupah copay schedules are published entitlement terms, not private quotes, so the stage structure and one kupah's example are now covered (item 31); full per-kupah tables stay out because they change and Meuhedet's are not published. Adoption remains a pointer-only next-cycle item.

- Personalized medical advice, diagnosis, medication or protocol choice (defer to the treating physician and IVF unit).
- Real-time price quotes for specific private clinics (cite bands only, confirm with the clinic or kupah).
- Step-by-step legal drafting of surrogacy or parentage agreements (refer to a family-law attorney and the approvals committee).
- Adoption, a distinct legal track under different law.
- Abortion or pregnancy-termination committees, a separate domain.
- Mental-health treatment delivery (point to support resources only; defer therapy to a mental-health navigator skill and hotlines).
