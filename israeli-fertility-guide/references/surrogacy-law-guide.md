# Israeli Surrogacy Law: Guide

General information on published rules only, not legal advice. See the legal notice in SKILL.md.

## Overview

Surrogacy in Israel is governed by the Embryo Carrying Agreements Law 1996 (חוק הסכמים לנשיאת עוברים). Every agreement must be approved by a statutory committee before treatment begins. This guide covers the framework and the process at the level the sources support; for anything specific to your case, the committee and a lawyer who specializes in surrogacy are the authorities.

## Who Can Use Surrogacy

| Point | Details |
|-------|---------|
| Genetic link | At least one intended parent must be genetically linked to the child |
| Eligible parties | Couples, a single intended mother and a single intended father, since the Supreme Court ruling in HCJ 781/15 (11.7.2021), implemented by Director General Circular 7/2021 (27.12.2021) |
| Residency and age | Intended parents are Israeli residents, at least 18, and at least one has not yet turned 54 at approval (the committee may waive the maximum age in special cases) |
| Existing children | A couple has no more than 2 common children; a single intended parent no more than 2 genetic children |
| Medical need | For an intended mother, a gynecological or medical opinion that she cannot conceive and carry a pregnancy, or that pregnancy may seriously endanger her health (section 4(a)(2) of the law) |
| Religion | The surrogate is of the same religion as the intended mother or the intended father (the committee may deviate when all parties are not Jewish) |
| Egg age | The committee will not approve the use of eggs (the intended mother's or a donor's) retrieved after age 43 |
| Two intended fathers | The sperm of only one intended parent is used in a given fertilization, and embryos from both may not be transferred in the same treatment cycle |
| Single intended mother | May use donor sperm, but may not use a donated egg for surrogacy; fertilization must use her own egg |
| Surrogate as egg donor | Never permitted |

## The Approval Committee

| Point | Details |
|-------|---------|
| Composition | Seven members, including a clergy member of the parties' religion |
| Sessions | Closed |
| Implementation guidance | Director General Circular 07/2021 |

## Surrogate Requirements

| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| Residency | Israeli resident |
| Age | Has turned 22 and not yet 39 when the agreement is approved |
| Previous births | Has given birth before |
| Relationship | Not a relative of the intended parents |

Other criteria (marital status, health evaluations, limits on repeat surrogacy) are set by the law and the committee; check the Ministry of Health service page and ask the lawyer rather than relying on a summary.

## The Process

1. Consult a lawyer who specializes in surrogacy law, and read the Ministry of Health and Ministry of Justice warnings before considering surrogacy abroad.
2. Identify a surrogate who meets the requirements above.
3. Complete the medical, psychological and legal documentation the committee requires.
4. Each side's lawyer handles the agreement. This guide does not draft or supply agreement terms.
5. Apply to the approval committee and wait for its decision before any treatment starts.
6. After approval, IVF and embryo transfer take place at a licensed unit in Israel. The intended parents' IVF stages are funded by their kupah within the basket entitlement for a first and second child; the kupah does not pay the surrogacy costs or the egg-donation costs (Maccabi states this for an intended father until his 54th birthday).
7. After the birth, legal parenthood is established through a legal procedure; have the lawyer explain it before signing anything.

There is no official timeline. Durations depend on finding a surrogate, preparing documentation and the committee's schedule.

## Costs

Surrogacy carries a large out-of-pocket cost: the surrogate's compensation, legal fees for both sides, escrow, insurance and medical expenses the basket does not cover. There is no official tariff, and the totals quoted online come mostly from law-firm marketing pages, so treat any single number as unverified and get written quotes.

## Embryos Fertilized Abroad

The law requires the agreement to provide that the IVF, the embryo transfer, the pregnancy and the birth take place in Israel. Under a temporary Ministry of Health decision, extended from time to time, eggs donated and fertilized at an MoH-recognised clinic abroad may be imported for surrogacy in Israel if the donation-and-fertilization contract was signed by 1.8.2027, with written confirmation of the signing date from the clinic before import (MoH Fertility Department letter of 21.7.2026). A memorandum of law to make this permanent was published for public comment on 8.7.2026; it is not law. Check the current status with the approvals committee.

## Surrogacy Abroad

The Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Justice publish standing warnings about surrogacy in specific countries. Bringing a child born abroad to Israel and establishing parenthood is a separate legal procedure. Read the warnings and consult a lawyer before signing anything.
