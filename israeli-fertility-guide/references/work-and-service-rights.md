# Work, Reserve-Duty, Career-Service and Bereavement Rights During Fertility Treatment

Detail behind SKILL.md Step 7. These are statutory or official rights and do not depend on which kupat cholim or SHABAN plan the person holds. Checked against Kol Zchut and Bituach Leumi pages on 2026-11-11.

## Absence from work (employees)

Source: Women's Employment Regulations (Absence for Fertility and IVF Treatments) 1990, via Kol Zchut "היעדרות מהעבודה וימי מחלה בגין טיפולי פוריות".

| Who | Quota | Charged against |
|-----|-------|-----------------|
| Woman, 5-day week | Up to 4 treatment series a year, up to 16 days each, 64 days a year | Accrued sick days |
| Woman, 6-day week | Up to 4 series, up to 20 days each, 80 days a year | Accrued sick days |
| Woman employed by the state (since 01.08.2018) | 2 extra days per series: 18 per series and up to 72 a year (5-day week), 22 and up to 88 (6-day week) | Accrued sick days |
| Man in treatment himself | 12 days a year | Accrued sick days |
| Any of the above, partial days | Up to 40 hours a year (pro-rated for part-time) taken as single hours, paid from the first hour | Accrued sick days |

**Pay for whole days.** The ladder is the same shape for both sexes, but it restarts at a different point:

- Women: it restarts for EACH treatment series. Day 1 of the series unpaid, days 2 and 3 at 50%, full pay from day 4.
- Men: it runs once per YEAR. Day 1 of the year unpaid, days 2 and 3 at 50%, full pay from day 4 through day 12. Any further fertility-absence day that year carries no sick pay.

**What the regulations do not give.** A partner who only accompanies the patient has no statutory absence right for that; only if the patient became fully dependent on others for daily activities may the partner use up to 6 sick days to care for them. When sick days run out, the employer may (but need not) advance sick pay against future accrual, and the employee may ask to have the absence charged to annual leave or ask for unpaid leave.

## Protection from dismissal (employees)

Source: Women's Employment Law, via Kol Zchut "איסור פיטורי עובד/ת במהלך טיפולי פוריות".

| Situation | Protection | Conditions |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Absent for treatment | No dismissal and no cut to pay or job scope during the absence and for 150 days after it ends, regardless of seniority | Advance notice to the employer with a medical certificate; treatment toward at most 2 births while employed at that employer, or toward 2 births with the current partner; fewer than 2 years since the first day of absence |
| In treatment but not absent | No dismissal during the treatment period or for 150 days from its start, whichever is later | At least 6 months with the employer; tell the employer of the treatment within 3 working days of the dismissal notice (or of the dismissal, if no notice was given), and hand over a medical certificate within 14 days |

Only the person in treatment is protected, not the partner. The only exception is a permit from the Women's Employment Law supervisor at the Ministry of Labor.

## Career soldiers (keva)

Source: Kol Zchut "איסור פיטורי חייל/ת משירות קבע במהלך טיפולי פוריות".

- No discharge during treatment absences and for 150 days after, provided a medical certificate was given to the commander and fewer than 2 years have passed since the first absence.
- No discharge for 150 days from the start of treatment even without absence, provided the commander is told within 7 days of the discharge notice and a medical certificate is delivered within 14 days.
- The protection covers treatment toward up to 2 births with the current partner.
- The exception is a permit from the Minister of Defense or his delegate, which is not given when the discharge is connected to the treatment, and cannot be retroactive.

## Reserve duty (miluim)

Three separate Kol Zchut pages, three different rules. Do not merge them.

| Who | Release | How to apply |
|-----|---------|--------------|
| Male reservist undergoing treatment himself | At least: clinic-based treatment, 2 days before the expected ovulation to 3 days after (5 days); IVF, 3 days before to 7 days after (10 days); IVF with surgical sperm retrieval, 3 days before the surgery to 3 months after it | Form 58 in the IDF reserves personal area, with a medical certificate giving the expected ovulation date |
| Male reservist whose partner is in treatment | Entitled to a release of at least the same 5 days (clinic) or 10 days (IVF). The surgical-retrieval period is not on this page | Form 58 with a certificate from the treating gynecologist giving the expected ovulation date |
| Female reservist in treatment | No fixed minimum periods are published. She presents a certificate from her treating gynecologist to the unit doctor, who decides on deferral or freezing of active service according to the circumstances | Through the unit doctor |

For the male tracks: a request filed at least 10 days before service starts is decided within 10 days, one filed less than 10 days before is decided by the day before service at the latest, and one filed during service within 72 hours. The male releases do not apply to an emergency call-up (Tzav 8); the female-reservist page does not address it.

## Bituach Leumi interface

- Fertility treatment itself carries no Bituach Leumi benefit; absence is paid as sick days under the regulations above.
- Once pregnant, the pregnancy-preservation benefit (shmirat heriyon) pays for a doctor-certified absence of at least 30 consecutive days (14 for a subsequent period), for employed and self-employed women meeting the contribution conditions. IVF pregnancies and OHSS are common triggers.
- Maternity benefit (dmei leda) eligibility does not depend on how the pregnancy was conceived.

## Bereaved families of victims of hostile acts

Source: Kol Zchut "השתתפות במימון טיפולי פוריות ופונדקאות להורים שכולים של חללי פעולת איבה" (figures stated there as correct for 2026).

- Who: bereaved parents (couples or individuals), widows, widowers and recognised partners of the victims, and orphans aged 21 and over.
- What: Bituach Leumi participates in funding fertility and surrogacy treatment, in Israel and abroad; surrogacy only through companies approved by the Ministry of Health.
- How much: up to 28,562 NIS in total for all treatments (2026 figure), doubled to 57,124 NIS for multiple loss. The amount is updated over time, so check the current figure.
- When: only after the kupah and SHABAN funding has been used, and the claim must be filed within one year of the actual payment, at the hostile-acts department of a Bituach Leumi branch.

This Bituach Leumi track is for families of victims of hostile acts. Bereaved families of IDF soldiers should ask the Ministry of Defense about its own fertility-funding terms; they are not described here.
